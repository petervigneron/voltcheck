## Battery replaced under the 2017–2022 recall

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| Which batteries | High voltage lithium-ion batteries and battery components installed under the High Voltage Battery safety recalls on 2017–2022 Bolt EV and 2022 Bolt EUV[^10][^11] |
| Warranty | 8-year/100,000-mile GM Service Replacement Parts Limited Warranty[^10][^11] |
| Clock starts | The date and mileage at the time of battery replacement[^10][^11] |
| Relation to the original warranty | Runs concurrent with any remaining term of the 8-year/100,000-mile New Vehicle Limited Warranty for Electric Vehicle components[^10][^11] |
| EV components installed under this warranty | "EV components installed under this special 8/100 (160,000 km) GM Service Replacement Part Warranty will continue to be warranted for the remainder of the 8/100 (160,000 km) GM Service Replacement Part warranty, or 12 months, whichever is greater."[^10][^11] |
| Also included | Courtesy Transportation and Roadside Assistance coverage for the term of the warranty[^10][^11] |
| Batteries replaced for any other reason | A customer-purchased battery gets the 3-year/100,000-mile Consumer Parts Warranty; a battery replaced under the New Vehicle Warranty gets the remainder of the 8-year/100,000-mile New Vehicle EV Component Limited Warranty or 12 months, whichever is greater[^10][^11] |
| Where it is recorded | Applied to the vehicle's record in GM's Applicable Warranties section of Investigate Vehicle History after the battery is installed and the recall campaign is closed[^10][^11]. GM told dealers to print a Bolt Warranty Certificate for the car's VIN and hand it to the owner, and to tell the owner the updated battery warranty can be seen in the Warranty Tracker in MyAccount[^12] |

## What is not covered

GM's booklets for 2017 through 2023 and for 2027 exclude from the New Vehicle Limited Warranty:

- Damage caused by collision, fire, theft, freezing, vandalism, riot, explosion, or objects striking the vehicle.[^2][^13][^9]
- Misuse of the vehicle, such as driving over curbs, overloading, racing or other competition.[^2][^13][^9]
- Alteration, modification or tampering with the vehicle after final assembly, including its software; installation of non-GM parts; water or fluid contamination.[^2][^13][^9]
- Any vehicle whose odometer has been disconnected or altered, or whose mileage cannot be determined; the warranty is void on vehicles currently or previously titled as salvaged, scrapped, junked, or otherwise considered a total loss.[^2][^13][^9]
- Damage caused by failure to follow the recommended maintenance schedule intervals.[^2][^13][^9]
- Economic loss and extra expense, including inconvenience, lodging and meals, loss of vehicle use, loss of time or pay, and storage.[^2][^13][^9]

The 2027 booklet adds:

- Damage to the battery from incompatible charging methods, or from an adapter or charger that General Motors does not sell or approve.[^9]
- Excessive use of the main battery for non-propulsion purposes, including semi-permanent or permanent daily power provisions. GM reserves the right to curtail non-propulsion use it determines is harmful to the battery.[^9]

## Scope note

US market, Bolt EV model years 2017 through 2023, Bolt EUV 2022 and 2023, and the 2027 Bolt, from the warranty documents Chevrolet's manuals page lists for each of those years (for 2017, the Bolt EV owner manual's warranty section); from 2020 on the booklets' warranty sections say "electric vehicles" rather than naming the Bolt, and the 2022 and 2023 booklets read are GM's current editions, dated January 2025 and December 2024 respectively, not the first printings. The recall replacement-battery warranty is a separate parts warranty that applies only to a car whose battery was replaced under the recall, and GM's per-VIN record is what shows whether a given car has one. The booklet delivered with a specific car is the authority for that car.
