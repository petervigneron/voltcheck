# Chevrolet Bolt EV & Bolt EUV (model years 2017–2023): Charging

## DC fast charging

| Model year | DC fast charging |
|---|---|
| 2017 Bolt EV | Optional (RPO CBT, fast charging accommodations) — available, not standard, on LT and Premier; check the car[^1]. The owner's manual titles the section DC Charging (If Equipped)[^2] |
| 2019 Bolt EV | Optional (RPO CBT, DC fast charging) — available on both trims; check the car[^3] |
| 2020 Bolt EV | Optional — the owner's manual titles the section DC Charging (If Equipped); check the car[^4] |
| 2022 Bolt EUV | Standard — the manual titles the section DC Charging, with no qualifier[^5] |
| 2023 Bolt EV | Standard — RPO CBT listed as standard equipment on 1LT and 2LT[^6] |

- The option is invisible in the model name and the trim, so a 2017–2021 car has to be checked individually.
- GM does not publish a peak kW figure for either car. Its documents specify the charger, not the car: at least 80 kW available in 2017 and 2020[^2][^4], then a current instead of a power from 2022 — 150 amps[^5][^7].
- Charging does not stop at the 30-minute mark: it continues to 100% at a gradually slower rate.[^7]
- Charging speed varies significantly with outside temperature.[^7]

| Model year | Charger GM specifies | Range added in about 30 minutes, from a depleted battery |
|---|---|---|
| 2017 Bolt EV | At least 80 kW available[^2] | 90 mi (145 km)[^2] |
| 2020 Bolt EV | At least 80 kW available[^4] | 90 mi (145 km)[^4] |
| 2022 Bolt EUV | Capable of 150 amps[^5] | Up to 95 mi (153 km)[^5] |
| 2023 Bolt EV | At least 150 amps available[^7] | Up to 100 mi[^7] |
| 2023 Bolt EUV | At least 150 amps available[^7] | Up to 95 mi[^7] |

## Home charging (Level 2)

| Model year | Onboard charger |
|---|---|
| 2017 Bolt EV | 7.2 kW — RPO K23, standard on LT and Premier[^1] |
| 2019 Bolt EV | 7.2 kW — RPO K23, standard on LT and Premier[^3] |
| 2023 Bolt EV | 11.5 kW — RPO K28, standard on 1LT and 2LT[^6] |

| Model year | 120V / 8A | 120V / 12A | 240V / 32A | 240V / 48A |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2017 Bolt EV | — | About 50 h to full, about 4 mi per hour[^2] | About 9.5 h to full, about 25 mi per hour[^2] | — |
| 2020 Bolt EV | — | About 55 h to full[^4] | About 10 h to full, about 25 mi per hour[^4] | — |
| 2022 Bolt EUV | About 79 h to full, about 2.8 mi per hour[^5] | About 55 h to full, about 4 mi per hour[^5] | About 10 h to full, about 25 mi per hour[^5] | About 7 h to full, about 37 mi per hour[^5] |
| 2023 Bolt EV & EUV | — | About 55 h to full[^7] | — | About 7 h to full[^7] |

- 32 amps is the Dual Level Charge Cord's maximum.[^5]
- GM's advice on home equipment: Level 2 equipment rated at least 240 volts / 48 amps gives the fastest home charging time.[^7]
- The cord in the trunk is not the fast one. RPO EJK, a 120-volt portable cord, is the standard cord in 2019 and 2023; the 120/240-volt Dual Level cord, RPO EKT, is an option that replaces it.[^3][^6]

## Connector and adapters

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| Tesla Supercharger access | Bolt EV customers can charge with GM's NACS DC adapter. The car still needs the DC fast-charging hardware above; the adapter does not add it[^8] |
| Adapter price and warranty | $225 MSRP, 12-month limited warranty from the date of purchase[^8] |
| Tesla Level 2 chargers | Not compatible. The NACS DC adapter is for Tesla Superchargers only[^8] |
| 2019 and 2020 Bolt EV | Chevrolet has a software update to help secure the charge-port lock while an adapter is in use, and directs those owners to a dealer service appointment[^8] |

## Scope note

Model years opened for this sheet: 2017, 2019, 2020, 2022 and 2023. Model years 2018 and 2021 were not checked, and a Bolt EV from either could have DC fast charging as an option in the same way. Every RPO-code claim above comes from a Bolt EV order guide, so it is a Bolt EV claim; the Bolt EUV existed only for 2022 and 2023, and every EUV figure comes from GM's 2022 EUV owner's manual or the 2023 "Get To Know" guide.
