# Ford Mustang Mach-E: Battery Warranty

## High voltage battery coverage

| Model year | Electric Vehicle Component coverage | High Voltage Battery Capacity Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first[^1] | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first; capacity below 70 percent of beginning-of-life capacity[^1] |
| 2022 | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first[^2] | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first; capacity below 70 percent of beginning-of-life capacity[^2] |
| 2023 | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first[^3] | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first; capacity below 70 percent of beginning-of-life capacity[^3] |
| 2024 | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first[^4] | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first; capacity below 70 percent of beginning-of-life capacity[^4] |
| 2025 | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first[^5] | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first; capacity below 70 percent of beginning-of-life capacity[^5] |
| 2026 | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first[^6] | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first; capacity below 70 percent of beginning-of-life capacity[^6] |

- The capacity coverage is in addition to the high voltage battery assembly's limited warranty for defects in materials or workmanship.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]

## Coverage at a glance

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| Components covered | The high voltage battery assembly, Bussed Electrical Center (BEC), Battery Energy Control Module (BECM), on-board charger, Inverter System Controller (ISC), DC/DC converter, and eDrive[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6] |
| Capacity floor | 70 percent of the high voltage battery's beginning-of-life capacity, as determined by an authorized EV Certified Ford Dealer[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6] |
| Capacity remedy | Ford covers the component repair or replacement necessary to correct the excessive capacity loss[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6] |
| Measurement | The measurement method, and the decision whether to repair, replace, or provide reconditioned or remanufactured parts, are at Ford's sole discretion[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6] |
| Replacement parts | New, factory remanufactured, or factory refurbished, at Ford's discretion; refurbished battery components align with the vehicle's age and mileage[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6] |
| Where repairs are done | An EV Certified Ford dealership[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6] |
| Transfers to a later owner | Yes. A buyer of a previously owned Mach-E is eligible for any remaining warranty coverages[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6] |
| Clock starts | The Warranty Start Date: the day of delivery of the new vehicle or the day it is first put into service, for example as a dealer demonstrator, whichever occurs first[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6] |
| Emissions warranty | None. Ford states that fully-electric vehicles are not eligible for any emissions warranties[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6] |

- The 2023 guide (March 2025 Fifth Printing)[^7] and the 2024, 2025 and 2026 guides[^4][^5][^6] add: for new retail sales on or after July 1, 2023 sold from a California retailer and/or to a California resident, the Warranty Start Date and mileage start no earlier than when the goods are delivered to the customer.

## What is not covered

Under "Damage Caused By," Ford's guides for every model year 2021 through 2026 exclude damage caused by:

- Accidents, collision or objects striking the vehicle, including driving through a car wash.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
- Theft, vandalism, or riot.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
- Fire or explosion.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
- Using contaminated or improper fluids.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
- Customer-applied chemicals or accidental spills.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
- Driving through water deep enough to cause water to be ingested into any component.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
- Immersion or flooding of the high voltage battery assembly.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
- Abuse or misuse of the vehicle or the high voltage battery pack, such as driving over curbs, overloading, racing, or using the vehicle as a permanent stationary power source.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
- Exceeding the load limits specified on the certification label.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
- Failing to observe and resolve vehicle warnings and indications of battery concerns within a reasonable amount of time, which the guides give as "typically 30 days."[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
- Failing to complete software updates within a reasonable amount of time, which the guides give as "typically 30 days"; the 2022 through 2026 guides name these Ford Power-Up software updates.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
- The use of incompatible charging devices or methods.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
- Modifications to the high voltage battery assembly, high voltage system, or associated wiring.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
- Exposure to direct or intense heat sources outside of normal use, such as collision repairs that subject the vehicle to paint booth conditions that exceed Ford's recommendations.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
- Allowing the high voltage battery assembly to remain fully discharged, or at a near zero state of charge, for more than 14 days.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
- Repairs performed by facilities and personnel not authorized by Ford.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
- Improper vehicle storage resulting in high voltage battery damage.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]

Every guide also states that the New Vehicle Limited Warranty does not cover:

- "vehicles that have ever been labeled or branded as dismantled, fire, flood, junk, rebuilt, reconstructed, or salvaged; this will void the New Vehicle Limited Warranty"[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
- "vehicles that have been determined to be a total loss by an insurance company; this will void the New Vehicle Limited Warranty"[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]
