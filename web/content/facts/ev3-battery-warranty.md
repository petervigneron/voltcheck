# Kia EV3: Battery Warranty

## Battery coverage by model year

| Model year | EV System Warranty | Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery Capacity Coverage | Covers capacity loss below | Repair returns the pack to at least |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2027 | 120 months or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first[^1] | 10 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first[^2] | 70% of original battery capacity[^2] | 70% of original battery capacity[^3] |

## Coverage at a glance

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| What the EV System Warranty covers | Electric Motor, High voltage Battery, Electric Power Control Unit (EPCU), On Board Charger (OBC)[^1] |
| Repair standard | If possible, the EV battery components are repaired or replaced and the original EV Battery is returned to the vehicle; if necessary, the EV Battery is replaced with a new or remanufactured Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery. A repair or replacement may not return the battery to an "as new" 100% capacity[^2][^3] |
| Transfers to a second owner | Yes. Any remaining portion of any warranty is fully transferable to subsequent owners except the 120 months/100,000 miles Power Train (Original Owner) warranty[^4] |
| Clock starts | Date of First Service: the earliest of delivery to the first retail purchaser, lease, or placement into service as a company vehicle such as a demonstrator, rental or fleet vehicle[^5] |
| Outside the EV System Warranty | Any other electrical components in the vehicle, such as (but not limited to) the traditional 12 volt car battery, alternator, or other starter components[^1] |

## What is not covered

Besides the manual's general exclusions, the 2027 manual excludes EV System and Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery damage or failure caused by:

- Exposing a vehicle to ambient temperatures above 122F (50C) for over 24 hours.[^6]
- Storing a vehicle in temperatures below -22F (-30C) for over seven days.[^6]
- Leaving your vehicle for over 14 days where the Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery reaches a zero or near zero state of charge.[^6]
- Physically damaging or intentionally attempting to reduce the life of the Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery.[^6]
- Exposing the Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery to contact with a direct flame.[^6]
- Immersing any portion of the Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery in water or fluids.[^6]
- Opening the Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery enclosure or having the Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery serviced by someone other than a Kia certified EV technician.[^6]
- Neglecting to follow correct charging procedures.[^6]
- Use of incompatible charging devices.[^6]
- Damage to the charge port and/or vehicle components caused by a public charger, non-Kia supplied charge cord, or other third party electric vehicle service equipment (EVSE).[^6]
- Incidental or consequential damage caused by the failure to repair an existing problem.[^6]
