# Ford F-150 Lightning (model years 2023–2025): Charging

## Battery packs

| Model year | Packs, in Ford's usable-energy wording |
|---|---|
| 2023 | 98 kWh Standard Range; 131 kWh Extended Range[^1] |
| 2024 | 98 kWh Standard Range; 131 kWh Extended Range[^2] |
| 2025 | 98 kWh Standard Range; 123 kWh Extended Range; 131 kWh Extended Range[^3] |

## DC fast charging

| Model year | Pack | 15–80% on a 150 kW charger | Miles added in 10 min on a 150 kW charger |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 98 kWh Standard Range | 44 min[^1] | 41 mi[^1] |
| 2023 | 131 kWh Extended Range | 41 min[^1] | 54 mi[^1] |
| 2024 | 98 kWh Standard Range | 32 min[^2] | 62 mi[^2] |
| 2024 | 131 kWh Extended Range | 38 min[^2] | 65 mi[^2] |
| 2025 | 98 kWh Standard Range | 32 min[^3] | 62 mi[^3] |
| 2025 | 123 kWh Extended Range | 38 min[^3] | 65 mi[^3] |
| 2025 | 131 kWh Extended Range | 38 min[^3] | 65 mi[^3] |

- Peak DC charging rate: not published. Ford states DC fast charging as a test condition — 150 kW, 15% to 80% — and its 2024 and 2025 order guides state a Level 2 peak rate for each configuration while stating no DC peak at all.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]
- Ford's tested window is 15% to 80%, not 10% to 80%.[^1][^2][^3]
- On a 50 kW charger, 15% to 80%, 2023: 91 minutes Standard Range, 122 minutes Extended Range.[^1]
- Ford's footnote on the 2023 miles-added table reads "Excludes Platinum models."[^1]

| Model year | Pack | 80A Charge Station Pro | 48A Connected Charge Station | 240V Mobile Charger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 98 kWh Standard Range | 10 h[^1] | 10 h[^1] | 13 h at 32A[^1] |
| 2023 | 131 kWh Extended Range | 8 h[^1] | 13 h[^1] | 19 h at 32A[^1] |
| 2024 | 98 kWh Standard Range | 10 h[^2] | 10 h[^2] | 15 h at 30A[^2] |
| 2024 | 131 kWh Extended Range | 13 h[^2] | 13 h[^2] | 20 h at 30A[^2] |

- All charge times above are 15% to 100%. Ford publishes one merged 48A/80A figure for 2024 and 2025, the 2025 131 kWh pack's 8 hours at 80A being the exception.[^2][^3]
- Est. 22.2 hours to charge at home with the Ford Mobile Power Cord, an average of 13-14 miles per charging hour — Ford's figure for the F-150 Lightning on a page naming no model year or pack.[^6]
- 9 hours with the Ford Charge Station Pro, an average of 30 miles per charging hour, stated on the same page without the estimated hedge.[^6]

| Model year | Charging equipment |
|---|---|
| 2023 | 30A Mobile Charger (120V/240V) included with both packs; 48A Connected Charge Station optional; 80A Charge Station Pro, the unit carrying Ford Intelligent Backup Power, optional with Standard Range and included with Extended Range[^1] |
| 2024 | Ford's order guide lists Charge Station Pro under Deleted in its Major Product Changes[^4] |
| 2025 | 30A Mobile Charger, 48A Connected Charge Station and Charge Station Pro all listed as Available Ford Accessory[^3] |

## Connector and adapters

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| Charge port | SAE J1772 CCS (Combo Connector System), capable of charging on 120V, 240V and DC fast charge, in both the 2024 and the 2025 order guide[^4][^5] |
| Tesla Supercharger access | The Ford Fast Charging Adapter gives F-150 Lightning owners access to Tesla's V3 and above Superchargers[^7] |
| Adapter part and price | Ford Fast Charging Adapter (NACS), part VRK9Z10E826A, $200.00 on Ford's accessories site, 3-year warranty from the purchase date[^8] |
| Adapter ratings | Up to 500 amps and 1,000 volts, IP67 weatherproofing, operation from -22°F to 122°F[^8] |
| AC charging through the adapter | Not compatible with Level 1 or Level 2 AC chargers, including Tesla Destination Chargers and home chargers with NACS connectors[^8] |
| Third-party adapters | Use only NACS DC adapters sold or provided by Ford or Tesla. Ford warns that not following its instructions "may result in vehicle damage not covered by the vehicle warranty"[^9] |
| Supercharger compatibility | Not all Tesla Superchargers are compatible with non-Tesla EVs; Ford directs owners to check in the Ford app[^10] |
