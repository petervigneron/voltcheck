# BMW X5 plug-in hybrid: Battery Warranty

## High-voltage battery limited warranty, all states

| Model year | Plug-in X5 | Term |
|---|---|---|
| 2016 | X5 xDrive40e | 8 years or 80,000 miles, whichever occurs first, for an X5 xDrive40e produced on or after 6/1/2015[^1] |
| 2017 | X5 xDrive40e | 8 years or 80,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^2] |
| 2018 | X5 xDrive40e | 8 years or 80,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^3] |
| 2019 | None | No plug-in X5 was offered[^4][^12] |
| 2020 | None | No plug-in X5 was offered[^5][^12] |
| 2021 | X5 xDrive45e | 8 years or 80,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^6] |
| 2022 | X5 xDrive45e | 8 years or 80,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^7] |
| 2023 | X5 xDrive45e | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever occurs first, in BMW's 2024 reissue of the 2023 booklet[^8] |
| 2024 | X5 xDrive50e | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^9][^13] |
| 2025 | X5 xDrive50e | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^10] |
| 2026 | X5 xDrive50e | 96 months (8 years) or 100,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^11] |

## California and ZEV-state term

| Model year | High-voltage (traction) battery | States |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 10 years or 150,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^6] | California, Connecticut, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, Rhode Island, Vermont[^6] |
| 2023 | 10 years or 150,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^8] | California, Colorado, Connecticut, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, Rhode Island, Vermont[^8] |
| 2024 | 10 years or 150,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^9] | California, Colorado, Connecticut, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, Rhode Island, Vermont[^9] |
| 2025 | 10 years or 150,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^10] | California, Colorado, Connecticut, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Minnesota, Nevada, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, Rhode Island, Vermont, Virginia, Washington[^10] |
| 2026 | None: BMW states the California TZEV program does not apply to 2026 plug-in hybrids[^11] | None |

- The longer term belongs to the California Emission Control System Limited Warranty TZEV, which covers cars certified to California's Transitional Zero Emission Vehicle standard; BMW states that certain X5 plug-ins of each of these years are.[^6][^7][^8][^9][^10]
- The certification is shown on the Vehicle Emission Control Information label in the engine compartment, after the word "California".[^6][^7][^8][^9]
- In 2021, 2023, 2024 and 2025 the 10-year battery term is the stated exception to that warranty's 15 years or 150,000 miles for emission-related parts.[^6][^8][^9][^10]

## Coverage at a glance

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is covered, 2016–2022 | The high-voltage lithium-ion battery assembly, against defects in materials or workmanship[^1][^2][^3][^6][^7] |
| What is covered, 2023–2025 | The high-voltage (traction) battery assembly, against defects in materials or workmanship[^8][^9][^10] |
| What is covered, 2026 | The high-voltage traction battery assembly and its internal components, against defects in materials and/or workmanship[^11] |
| Capacity coverage | The 2026 booklet's coverage for capacity below 70 percent applies to battery electric models, not plug-in hybrids[^11] |
| Repair standard, 2016–2025 | Repair or replacement of the defective part(s) with new or authorized remanufactured parts, at BMW NA's sole choice[^1][^2][^3][^6][^7][^8][^9][^10] |
| Repair standard, 2026 | A software update, or repair and/or replacement of the appropriate HV battery components with new, refurbished and/or reconditioned parts, at BMW NA's sole choice[^11] |
| Transfers to a second owner, 2016–2025 | Yes. The battery warranty is part of the New Vehicle Limited Warranty, which BMW NA gives to the first retail purchaser and each subsequent purchaser[^1][^2][^3][^6][^7][^8][^9][^10] |
| Clock starts, 2016–2025 | The date of first retail sale or the date the vehicle is first placed into service as a sales demonstrator, Aftersales Mobility Program (AMP) Vehicle or company vehicle, whichever is earlier[^1][^2][^3][^6][^7][^8][^9][^10] |
| Where it is valid | U.S.-specification vehicles, serviced at an authorized BMW center in the United States, including Puerto Rico[^1][^2][^3][^6][^7][^8][^9][^10][^11] |

## What is not covered

The 2026 high-voltage battery warranty's "What Is Not Covered" section lists:

- Issues caused by not adhering to the Owner's Manual instructions regarding the high-voltage battery (especially instructions about its service life), or issues/damage caused by not charging the BEV model within 14 days after the charge level indicator shows "0 % / 0 km (mile)" range remaining.[^11]
- Issues/damage caused by using non-BMW approved third-party charging equipment (charging cables, charging plugs) and/or defective/damaged charging infrastructure equipment, such as public charging stations.[^11]
- HV battery assembly physical damage caused by an external heat source, fire/open flame, vandalism, accident, storm, flooding and/or lightning strike, and damage due to an act of God.[^11]
- Immersion of the HV battery assembly and/or parts of it in water or other liquids, including direct exposure to high-pressure cleaning devices (except automated car washes).[^11]
- Exposing the HV battery to a corrosive environment, for example a highly acidic or highly alkaline environment and/or cleaning agents.[^11]
- Non-compliance with the BEV model's load limits and/or towing capacities, when applicable, as specified by BMW.[^11]
- Use and operation of the BEV model as an off-board power source for functions other than driving the vehicle, its factory-installed equipment and/or BMW approved accessories.[^11]
- Use and operation of the HV battery after its removal from the vehicle.[^11]
- Repairs of non-genuine BMW parts, or of damage resulting from their use.[^11]
- The warranty is null and void for specific vehicle components that were previously replaced with used or salvaged automobile parts, including repairs of any damage resulting from the use of these parts; if the odometer has been replaced or altered and the true mileage cannot be determined, or the VIN is altered or cannot be determined; if the vehicle has been declared a total loss or sold for salvage; or if it has been used in any competitive event activities (for example, overstraining the HV battery).[^11]
