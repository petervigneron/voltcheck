# Volkswagen ID. Buzz (model year 2025, US market): Charging

## DC fast charging

- Peak DC rate: 200 kW, standard on every trim — Pro S, Pro S Plus RWD, Pro S Plus 4Motion, 1st Edition RWD, 1st Edition 4Motion. Volkswagen's order-guide line: "200 kW DC fast charging capability - requires compatible public charging station."[^1]
- Time to charge 10% to 80%: about 26 minutes. Volkswagen's words: "The 91-kWh battery can be charged at a rate of up to 200 kW at DC fast-charging stations, allowing for 10 to 80 percent charging in about 26 minutes."[^2]
- Battery: 91 kWh gross, standard on every trim, with 13 cell modules. Volkswagen prints the qualifier itself on the order guide's equipment chart: "91 kWh (gross) lithium-ion battery pack w/ Performance Drive Unit." The press kit's "a 91-kWh battery" is the same pack with the word left off.[^1][^2]

## Preconditioning

- The ID. Buzz preconditions its battery automatically when you navigate to a charger. Volkswagen: "When the native navigation's route guidance with the Electric Vehicle Route Planner is active, pre-conditioning is started automatically on the way to the next quick-charging station."[^2]
- It can also be started by hand: "Without active route guidance, the function can also be manually activated using the charging menu in the Infotainment system."[^2]
- What it buys: Volkswagen states "The battery is heated to the optimum temperature before the charging stop so it can accept energy at the maximum rate. This enables the charging time to be reduced by several minutes, particularly in winter."[^2]

## Connector

- No Volkswagen document opened for this sheet names the ID. Buzz's port type outright. What Volkswagen does state is what its adapter converts to, for these two vehicles: a "DC fast-charging adapter for North American Charging System (NACS) to Combined Charging System (CCS) port," in a release whose subject is ID.4 and ID. Buzz owners.[^3] The CCS answer on this page rests on that sentence, not on a spec line.
- Tesla Supercharger access opened to ID. Buzz owners on November 18, 2025, and requires a Volkswagen-approved NACS adapter.[^3]
- Adapter MSRP is $200, sold by Volkswagen dealers and at parts.vw.com. Original owners of model year 2025 ID. Buzz vans are eligible for a $100 rebate on the adapter. Volkswagen's terms: "All rebate claims must be submitted within 90 days of the adapter purchase date and no later than July 15, 2026" — the adapter's purchase date, not the van's. An adapter comes standard with every model year 2026 Volkswagen EV.[^3]
- The adapter is for DC fast charging only. Volkswagen states it is not designed for Level 1 or Level 2 AC equipment, including home chargers and Tesla Destination Chargers.[^3]
- Volkswagen states that owners of certain 2024 and 2025 vehicles "will receive information about a recommended software update to support optimal charging behavior," and strongly recommends updating the vehicle's software before using the adapter.[^3]

## AC (Level 2, home) charging

- Onboard AC charger: 11 kW, standard on every trim. Volkswagen's order-guide line: "11 kW AC charging capability (charging cable sold separately)."[^1]
- The charging cable is not included. Volkswagen lists a 2-in-1 (240V/120V) EV charging cable as a separate accessory.[^1]

## The included charging plan does not transfer

- Volkswagen says it in one word. Its own disclosure for the 2025 ID. Buzz Charging Plan: "Non-transferable." The same paragraph adds "Not available for commercial use, such as ride-hailing and ridesharing."[^2]
- The plan itself: 500 kWh of charging at Electrify America plus three years of Electrify America's Pass+ membership, which Volkswagen says saves about 25% against Guest and Pass base rates.[^2] Volkswagen's disclosure states the terms precisely: the plan "is included with new model year 2025 Volkswagen ID. Buzz vehicles purchased from an authorized U.S.-based Volkswagen dealer," and "Thirty-six (36) months of Pass+ membership and 500 kWh of complimentary DC Fast and L2 charging begins upon vehicle purchase."[^2]
- Plug&Charge on the ID. Buzz is tied to the original owner. Volkswagen's own terms: the feature "is available for use upon purchase of a new, unused 2025 ID. Buzz EV and can only be utilized by the original purchaser/owner for the duration of the included charging plan. Not valid for subsequent owners."[^2]
- The three-year plan begins at vehicle purchase, and Volkswagen states that "Functionality will deactivate upon expiration of the included 3-year charging plan."[^2] A second owner of a 2025 ID. Buzz does not get this.
- Volkswagen also states Plug&Charge "can only be used at Electrify America DC fast charging and Level 2 public charging stations."[^2]

## Scope note

Model year 2025 only, US market. Volkswagen's own 2025 order guide and 2025 press kit are the sources for every vehicle figure, and its November 2025 release for everything about the adapter. No other model year was checked, and none is claimed — the only 2026 statement on this page is Volkswagen's own, that an adapter ships with every 2026 Volkswagen EV.

The charging-plan and Plug&Charge terms are quoted from Volkswagen's own disclosure text on the press kit. They describe the plan as sold with a new 2025 van; nothing here should be read as describing what a second owner receives, beyond Volkswagen's explicit statement that the feature is "Not valid for subsequent owners."
