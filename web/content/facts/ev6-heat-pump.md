# Kia EV6: Heat Pump by Model Year and Trim

## Heat pump by model year and trim

| Model year | Trim | Heat pump |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | Light | None[^1] |
| 2022 | Wind, GT-Line | Optional — check the car[^1] |
| 2023 | Wind, GT-Line | Optional — check the car[^2] |
| 2023 | GT | Standard[^2] |
| 2024–2026 | Light Short Range | None[^3][^4][^5] |
| 2024–2026 | Light Long Range, Wind, GT-Line | Optional — check the car[^3][^4][^5] |
| 2024–2026 | GT | Standard[^3][^4][^5] |

## How to check an individual car

- The refrigerant label is on the underside of the hood, and Kia says it carries the amount of refrigerant.[^6][^7]
- With a heat pump the air conditioning charge is 850±25 g of R-1234yf; without one, 750±25 g.[^8]
- Kia's caveat: the label in the vehicle may differ from the illustration.[^7]

## Scope note

Model years 2022 through 2026, US market, each read from Kia America's own Features & Options file for that model year. Trim lineups change year to year and are listed as Kia lists them: the Light disappears for 2023 and returns for 2024 split into Light Short Range and Light Long Range, and the GT arrives for 2023. What "Optional" meant in practice — whether the heat pump could be ordered on its own or arrived only bundled — is not stated in any Kia document, so a Wind, a GT-Line or a Light Long Range has to be checked car by car.
