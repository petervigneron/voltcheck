# Kia EV6 (2022–2026 model years verified): Charging

## Connector by model year

- Model year 2025 ships with a native NACS port. Kia's own words: "2025 model year Kia EV6 and 2026 model year EV9 come standard with NACS charging ports."[^1]
- Model years 2022, 2023, and 2024 ship with a CCS1 port instead. Kia names the 2024 EV6 among the cars its NACS-to-CCS1 adapter programme is for, and treats every EV6 delivered before September 4, 2024 the same way. Kia's own words: "Customers who take delivery of a new 2024 or 2025 Kia EV9 or 2024 Kia EV6 from September 4, 2024, and later, will receive an adapter free of charge that will allow them access to more than 16,500 NACS DC fast chargers in the U.S." And: "Kia EV6, EV9 and Niro EV customers who took delivery prior to September 4, 2024, will have the opportunity to purchase an adapter from an authorized Kia dealer at a later date."[^3]
- Those two Kia statements are what the 2022-2024 CCS1 claim rests on, taken together: Kia dates the native NACS port to model year 2025, and puts the 2024 and earlier EV6 in the group that needs an adapter to reach one. Kia does not publish a per-model-year connector row anywhere — its own EV6 specification sheets have no charge-port-type row in any of the five years.[^1][^3][^4][^5][^6][^7][^8]
- Kia's own 2025 EV6 owner's manual confirms the port direction from the other side. It documents a "CCS1 adapter (if equipped)" and instructs the driver: "Insert the CCS1 adapter into the NACS vehicle, then check the connection status between the charger and the vehicle at the charging station." A CCS1 adapter is what a NACS-port car needs, not what a CCS1-port car needs.[^2]
- Kia offers three adapters. Kia's own words: "Three types of NACS adapters will be available: NACS to CCS1, CCS1 to NACS and J1772 to NACS. NACS to CCS1 allows existing Kia EVs to DC fast charge. The CCS1 coupler to NACS port enables NACS-equipped Kia EVs to charge on existing CCS DC chargers, while the J1772 to NACS adapter allows NACS-equipped Kia EVs to AC-charge on existing Level 2 chargers."[^3]
- The adapter was free to some buyers and not others — see the delivery-date cut-off quoted above. Kia does not publish a price for the purchased adapter in either release cited here, so none is stated.[^3]
- Charge port door location: rear, left side. Standard on every 2025 trim.[^7]
- The 2026 EV6's port type is not stated in any Kia document opened for this sheet. See the scope note.

## DC fast charging: 2022–2024

- Kia's figures are keyed to the charger, not to a peak the car advertises. Maximum charging input power on DC fast charge is 180 kW on the 58 kWh pack and 240 kW on the 77.4 kWh pack, model years 2022 and 2024. Kia's 2023 sheet lists 240 kW on all three of the trims it covers.[^4][^5][^6]
- 2022. Kia's sheet lists three trims: Light (58 kWh / 522.7V), Wind, and GT-Line (both 77.4 kWh / 697V). On a 50 kW EVSE (max 125A): 63 minutes on Light, 73 minutes on Wind and GT-Line. On a 350 kW EVSE (max 200A): 18 minutes on all three.[^4]
- 2023. Kia's sheet lists three trims — Wind, GT-Line, and GT — all on the 77.4 kWh / 697V pack. On a 50 kW EVSE (max 125A): 73 minutes. On a 350 kW EVSE (max 200A): 18 minutes.[^5]
- 2024. Kia's sheet lists five trims: a base short-range trim (58.0 kWh / 522.7V) and Light Long Range, Wind, GT-Line, and GT (all 77.4 kWh / 697V). On a 50 kW EVSE (max 125A): 63 minutes on the base trim, 73 minutes on the other four. On a 350 kW EVSE (max 200A): 18 minutes on all five.[^6]
- Kia calls that base 2024 trim two different things in two of its own specification documents. The one kiamedia links from its 2024 EV6 page today heads the column plain "Light"; another 2024 EV6 specification document, still served from kiamedia, heads it "Light Short Range." Every charging figure is identical between the two — only the column heading differs. A 2024 listing may carry either name.[^6]

## DC fast charging: 2025–2026

- The 2025 refresh changed the packs. The base trim's pack grew from 58.0 kWh to 63.0 kWh (523V), and the long-range pack grew from 77.4 kWh to 84.0 kWh (697V).[^7][^8]
- Maximum charging input power on DC fast charge: 180 kW on the base trim, 240 kW on Light Long Range, Wind, GT-Line, and (2025 only) GT.[^7][^8]
- 2025, on a 50 kW EVSE: 61 minutes on the base trim, 81 minutes on the other four. On a 150 kW EVSE: 24 minutes on the base trim, 29 minutes on the other four. On a 350 kW EVSE: 20 minutes on the base trim, Light Long Range, Wind, and GT-Line, and 18 minutes on GT.[^7]
- The 2025 base trim has two names too, the opposite way round. The document kiamedia links from its 2025 EV6 page today heads the column "Light Standard Range"; another 2025 EV6 specification document, still served, heads it plain "Light." Again every charging figure is identical between the two. So the bare word "Light" heads the 58.0 kWh car in one 2024 document and the 63.0 kWh car in one 2025 document — match a listing on its model year and pack, not on the word.[^6][^7]
- 2026, on a 50 kW EVSE: 61 minutes on Light, 81 minutes on Light Long Range, Wind, and GT-Line. On a 150 kW EVSE: 24 minutes on Light, 29 minutes on the other three. On a 350 kW EVSE: 20 minutes on all four. The 2026 specification sheet lists no GT trim.[^8]

### What Kia does not say, and one figure that does not line up

- Kia's specification sheets give these DC times with no state-of-charge window attached. They are not labelled 10%–80%, or 0%–80%, or anything else, in any of the five sheets.[^4][^5][^6][^7][^8]
- Kia's 2025 EV6 Vehicle Feature Tips document does give a window, in its own words: "You can charge from a 10% to 80% state of charge in about 18 minutes using a DC fast charger."[^9]
- That 18-minute figure matches the 2022, 2023, and 2024 specification sheets' 350 kW time. It does not match the 2025 sheet, which gives 20 minutes at 350 kW for four of the five 2025 trims and 18 minutes only for GT. Both documents are Kia's. This sheet reports the conflict rather than picking a winner.[^7][^9]

## AC (Level 2, home) charging

- Onboard charger: 10.9 kW. Standard on every trim, every model year 2022 through 2026.[^4][^5][^6][^7][^8]
- On an 11 kW EVSE at 240V/48A: 2022 through 2024, 5 hours 50 minutes on the 58.0 kWh pack and 7 hours 10 minutes on the 77.4 kWh pack. 2025 and 2026, 5 hours 40 minutes on the 63.0 kWh pack and 7 hours 20 minutes on the 84.0 kWh pack.[^4][^5][^6][^7][^8]
- On the in-cable control box at 120V/12A: 2022 through 2024, 51 hours 5 minutes on the 58.0 kWh pack and 68 hours on the 77.4 kWh pack. 2025 and 2026, 54 hours 5 minutes on the 63.0 kWh pack and 72 hours on the 84.0 kWh pack.[^4][^5][^6][^7][^8]
- Kia's 2025 Vehicle Feature Tips gives a Level 2 figure of its own, for a different endpoint. Kia's own words: "A Level 2 or 240-volt charger is the recommended charging system for your EV. This type of charger uses 240 volts of electricity and takes approximately 9 hours for a full charge." That is a **full** charge; the specification sheets' 11 kW times state no state-of-charge window at all. The two are not the same measurement and Kia does not equate them, so neither is offered here as a check on the other.[^9]

## Preconditioning

- Battery conditioning is a driver-controlled mode on the 2025 EV6, and Kia marks it "if equipped."[^2]
- What it does, in Kia's own words: "Battery conditioning mode is a feature that helps maintain charging performance at room temperature levels when the temperature of the high-voltage battery is high or low, such as in hot summers or cold winters."[^2]
- How it is switched, in Kia's own words: "A switch on the infotainment screen shows the battery conditioning mode status and allows it to be turned on and off."[^2]
- Navigation ties into it. Kia's own words: "When setting a DC charging station as a waypoint or destination in the navigation system, optimizing the battery temperature during the journey can reduce charging time to room temperature depending on the required time."[^2]
- It can also be started from a phone, with a time limit. Kia's own words: "The battery conditioning mode can be activated remotely using a smartphone app during vehicle parking. In this case, the battery conditioning mode will end either when the battery temperature reaches the appropriate range or after 30 minutes."[^2]
- It costs range. Kia's own words: "Using energy from the battery to optimize the temperature during this process may reduce the driving range."[^2]
- It is not guaranteed to work. Kia's own words: "Sufficient time may be required to optimize battery temperature, and conditioning may not work to secure driving range if the charge is low."[^2]
- Which 2025 trims are "equipped" is not stated. Kia's 2025 EV6 Features & Options sheet has no battery-conditioning row at all, so no trim breakdown is given here.[^10]

## Scope note

Model years 2022 through 2026 verified against Kia's own US specification sheets, which kiamedia.com publishes as spreadsheets rather than PDFs. Reading them as spreadsheet cells rather than as extracted PDF text is why the per-trim columns above can be stated per trim: each figure is tied to a named trim column in the file itself.

For model years 2024 and 2025 there are two Kia specification documents apiece, and kiamedia's own model-year pages now link a different one of each pair than this page originally cited. Both are cited above, because both are still served and a reader may land on either. The charging figures are identical across each pair, checked cell by cell. The base trim's column heading is not: for 2024 the two documents read "Light" and "Light Short Range," for 2025 "Light Standard Range" and "Light." Neither file carries an internal revision date, so this page does not say which of a pair came first — only which one kiamedia links today. It is also why the base trim is named by its pack rather than by the word "Light" wherever the 2024 and 2025 figures sit near each other: bare "Light" heads a 58.0 kWh column in one document and a 63.0 kWh column in another.

Preconditioning is verified against the 2025 owner's manual only. No 2022, 2023, 2024, or 2026 EV6 owner's manual was retrievable as a file — Kia's US owner's-manual portal serves a web viewer, not a downloadable PDF, and the mirror that carries the 2025 manual has no other EV6 year. Nothing in the preconditioning section should be read as applying to any model year other than 2025.

The 2025 owner's manual came from a mirror, cdn.dealereprocess.org, not from Kia's own host. Its cover page reads "2025 EV6 Owner's Manual" over the Kia wordmark, and its interior page numbering and figure IDs match Kia's own convention.

The 2026 EV6's charge-port type is not claimed. Kia's April 2025 release names the 2025 EV6 and the 2026 EV9 as NACS-standard and does not mention the 2026 EV6, and no 2026 EV6 owner's manual was retrievable. The 2026 charging figures above come from Kia's 2026 specification sheet and are stated without a connector claim attached.

The EV6 GT is included wherever Kia's own sheets list it, which is model years 2023 through 2025. The 2026 specification sheet lists no GT.
