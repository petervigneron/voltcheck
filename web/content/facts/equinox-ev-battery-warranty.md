# Chevrolet Equinox EV: Battery Warranty

## Coverage at a glance

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is covered | The propulsion battery pack and its internal components[^1][^2][^3][^4] |
| Term | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first[^1][^2][^3][^4] |
| Capacity floor | The battery is replaced or repaired if capacity falls below 75% of its original value during the warranty period, as determined by a certified dealer[^1][^2][^3][^4] |
| Replacement battery | One appropriate for the age and mileage of the vehicle. Chevrolet repairs or replaces the propulsion battery with new or refurbished components at its own discretion[^1][^2][^3][^4] |
| Transfers to a second owner | Yes[^1][^2][^3][^4] |
| Clock starts | The date the vehicle is first delivered or put in use[^1][^2][^3][^4] |
| Cost of a covered repair | No charge, including towing, parts and labor[^1][^2][^3][^4] |
| Bumper-to-bumper coverage | 3 years or 36,000 miles, whichever comes first[^1][^2][^3][^4] |

| Model year | Transfer | Where warranty repairs are made | Towing for a warranted electric-specific defect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | Transferable at no cost to any subsequent person who assumes ownership within the 8 years or 100,000 miles term, with no deductibles[^1] | An EV Certified Chevrolet dealer facility[^1] | Covered to the nearest EV Certified Chevrolet dealer during the 8-year/100,000-mile period if the vehicle cannot be driven[^1] |
| 2025 | Provided to the original owner and any subsequent owners of the vehicle during the warranty period[^2] | An authorized Chevrolet dealership[^2] | |
| 2026 | Provided to the original owner and any subsequent owners of the vehicle during the warranty period[^3] | An authorized Chevrolet dealership[^3] | |
| 2027 | Provided to the original owner and any subsequent owners of the vehicle during the warranty period[^4] | An authorized Chevrolet dealership[^4] | |

## Uses of the battery that stay covered

| Model years | Use | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 2025–2027 | Backup power during an outage | Covered as normal use[^2][^3][^4] |
| 2025–2027 | Selling energy back to the electrical grid | Covered as reasonable use; additional vehicle and in-home hardware may be required[^2][^3][^4] |
| 2025–2027 | Mobile power source — job-site power, vehicle-to-vehicle charging | Covered as reasonable use[^2][^3][^4] |

## California zero-emissions coverage

| Model year | Coverage | Term |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 | High Voltage Battery Warranty[^5] | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first[^5] |
| 2026 | Propulsion-related part short-term defects warranty[^5] | 3 years or 50,000 miles, whichever comes first[^5] |
| 2026 | Propulsion-related part long-term defects warranty, on the propulsion-related parts GM marks as exceeding the cost threshold for high priced warranted parts at the time of certification[^5][^6] | 7 years or 70,000 miles, whichever comes first[^5][^6] |

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| State-of-health floor | GM warrants to the ultimate purchaser and each subsequent purchaser that the battery is free from defects in materials and workmanship which cause the battery state of health to deteriorate to less than 70%, for 8 years or 100,000 miles. GM calls this the high voltage battery warranty[^6] |
| Parts covered beyond the standard warranty period | The entries GM marks with an asterisk on its California Propulsion Related Parts Warranty List — the battery energy control module, high voltage cables, front drive unit, rear drive unit, drive motor power inverter, battery charger coupler, battery charger, valve assembly with electronic brake control module, wheel drive shaft, radiator, radiator surge tank, vehicle electronics coolant pump, vehicle battery coolant pump, chiller, battery heater module, coolant thermal management hoses and pipes, coolant thermal management valves, and coolant refrigerant fan[^6] |
| Parts CARB names | Propulsion-related parts may include parts such as the electric drive motor, inverter, high voltage battery, onboard charger, and associated electronic control units, wiring and sensors[^6] |
| Cars it applies to | A vehicle certified for sale in California, per its emission control information label, that is serviced by an authorized Chevrolet or GM dealership in California or in a state that has specifically adopted California's emission warranty requirements — presently Massachusetts, New York, Oregon, Vermont and Washington[^6] |
| Vehicle weight limit | Under 10,000 lb GVWR[^5] |
| Cost to the owner | Repairs are at no cost, including diagnosis, parts and labor[^6] |

## What is not covered

| Model years | Excluded |
|---|---|
| 2024–2027 | Collision, fire, theft, freezing, vandalism, riot, explosion, or objects striking the vehicle[^7][^8][^9][^10] |
| 2024–2027 | Misuse such as driving over curbs, overloading, racing or other competition[^7][^8][^9][^10] |
| 2024–2027 | Installation of non-GM parts, and alteration, modification or tampering with the vehicle — body, chassis, software or other components — after final assembly[^7][^8][^9][^10] |
| 2024–2027 | Water or fluid contamination[^7][^8][^9][^10] |
| 2024–2027 | Damage from failing to follow the recommended maintenance schedule intervals, or from failing to use or maintain the fluids, lubricants and refrigerants the owner's manual recommends[^7][^8][^9][^10] |
| 2024–2027 | The warranty is void on a vehicle currently or previously titled as salvaged, scrapped, junked or otherwise considered a total loss[^7][^8][^9][^10] |
| 2025–2026 | Incompatible charging methods, or use of an adapter that is not sold or approved by General Motors, that could cause damage to the vehicle's battery[^8][^9] |
| 2027 | Incompatible charging methods, or use of an adapter or charger that is not sold or approved by General Motors, that could cause damage to the vehicle's battery[^10] |
| 2025–2027 | Damage resulting from failing to install a Chevrolet-provided over-the-air software update within 45 days of its availability to the vehicle[^8][^9][^10] |
| 2025–2027 | Excessive use of the main battery for non-propulsion purposes, including semi-permanent or permanent daily power provisions. General Motors reserves the right to curtail excessive non-propulsion use it determines to be harmful to the battery[^8][^9][^10] |

## Scope note

Chevrolet writes one electric-vehicle warranty booklet per model year rather than one per model, and the 2024, 2025, 2026 and 2027 editions are the ones read here; none of them names the Equinox EV. The California zero-emissions rows above come from the 2026 edition alone, and the 2024–2027 exclusion rows use the 2024 booklet's wording, which the later booklets broaden. The booklet delivered with a specific car is the authority for that car.
