# Nissan Ariya (model years 2023–2025, US market): Heat Pump

## 2023–2025

No Nissan document published for the US-market Ariya says the car has a heat pump. Twelve Nissan documents were opened for this sheet — three owner's manuals, three brochures, three full press-kit specification sets, and three Quick Reference Guides — and the word "pump" does not appear in any of them in connection with the Ariya's climate system, with one conditional boilerplate exception noted below that asserts nothing.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6][^7][^8][^10][^12]

Nissan's own enumeration of the Ariya's high-voltage components names a resistive heater in the heating position, not a heat pump. The manual's twelve-item list, verbatim: high-voltage wire harnesses (colored orange), high voltage junction box, service plug, On Board Charger (OBC), inverter, traction motor, DC/DC converter, air conditioner compressor, PTC heater, Li-ion battery, rear inverter (AWD models), rear traction motor (AWD models). The same list, in the same order, appears in the 2023, 2024, and 2025 manuals.[^1][^2][^3]

## What Nissan does list

- Battery heater. Standard on every trim for 2023 and 2024: Nissan's press-kit specification tables mark the "Battery heater" row "S" across the full trim span in both years, and the 2024 brochure grid marks it standard in all five trim columns (Venture+, Engage, Evolve+, Empower+, Platinum+). The 2023 and 2025 brochures carry the same "Battery heater" row; their per-trim cells were not read at the cell level.[^4][^5][^6][^7][^8]
- Dual-zone Automatic Temperature Control. Standard across the trim span.[^7][^8]
- Heated front seats, heated steering wheel, heated outboard rear seats, and climate-controlled (heated and cooled) front seats, each broken out by trim.[^5]

## How the absence was tested

- Nissan's 2023 press kit for the Ariya carries 519 specification table rows across 46 tables; the word "pump" occurs zero times in its text. The 2024 press kit carries 532 rows, also zero.[^7][^8]
- The 2023, 2024, and 2025 owner's manuals were searched in full. Neither "heat pump" nor "heat-pump" occurs in any of the three.[^1][^2][^3]
- The 2023, 2024, and 2025 brochures were searched in full. Neither phrase occurs in any of the three.[^4][^5][^6]
- Nissan's 2025 Ariya press kit was checked the same way: 527 specification table rows, and the word "pump" occurs zero times in its text. Its specification table carries a "Battery heater" row marked "S", the same granularity as the 2023 and 2024 kits.[^10]
- Control: Nissan's US newsroom does use the words, unhedged, when a Nissan has one. For the 2026 Leaf, Nissan writes: "To further optimize energy usage during cold conditions, LEAF has a standard, energy-efficient heat pump to help warm the cabin," and the 2026 LEAF Press Kit's specification grid heads a row "Hybrid heater system (heat pump)". The term is in Nissan USA's own vocabulary, and it reaches its spec grids as well as its prose; it is simply not applied to the Ariya.[^9][^11]
- One place the words do appear near the Ariya, and settle nothing: Nissan's Ariya Quick Reference Guides for 2023, 2024 and 2025 each carry a cold-weather note reading "Using the climate control system to heat the cabin when the outside temperature is below 32°F (0°C) uses more electricity (heat pump, if so equipped) and affects vehicle range more than using the heater at temperatures above 32°F (0°C)." It is conditional on its face — "if so equipped" — and it is shared Nissan-EV boilerplate rather than an Ariya statement: the identical sentence appears verbatim in Nissan's Leaf Quick Reference Guides for 2023 and 2025. It asserts nothing about either car.[^12][^13]

## Scope note

Model years 2023, 2024, and 2025, US market. Model year 2026 is not covered; no 2026 Ariya brochure, owner's manual, or press kit was opened for this sheet.

Twelve US Nissan documents were checked, all of them re-opened and searched this pass rather than carried over: the 2023, 2024 and 2025 owner's manuals, brochures, press kits and Quick Reference Guides. Controls were run in the same pass to prove the searches were live — "PTC heater" is found in all three manuals, "Battery heater" in all three brochures and all three press kits — so a zero result for "pump" is a finding rather than a failed search.

What is claimed here is precise, and the difference matters. Nissan nowhere states that the Ariya lacks a heat pump. What is stated above is that no Nissan document published for the US Ariya says it has one, that the single Nissan document enumerating the car's high-voltage components names a PTC heater in the heating position, and that Nissan's US newsroom uses the words "heat pump" plainly for a different Nissan when that car has one.

Non-US Ariya builds are out of scope entirely. Nissan sells the Ariya in markets with different equipment, and no non-US document was checked, so nothing here should be read as applying outside the United States.

Aggregator listings and enthusiast forums do describe the Ariya as heat-pump equipped. None of them was used as a source here, and none of them is contradicted by name — this page reports only what Nissan's own documents contain. Nissan does say it plainly for the **European** Ariya: nissan.co.uk's Ariya price-and-specifications page lists "Heat pump" as standard equipment in every grade and battery configuration it configures. That is a different car by specification — the same page lists a 22 kW onboard charger the US Ariya does not have — and it is out of scope here for the same reason every other non-US document is.
