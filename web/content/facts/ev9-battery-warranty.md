# Kia EV9: Battery Warranty

## Coverage at a glance

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is covered | Two warranties: the EV System Warranty (Electric Motor, High voltage Battery, Electric Power Control Unit, On Board Charger) and Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery Capacity Coverage[^1][^2][^8] |
| Term | 120 months (10 years), both coverages[^1][^2][^8] |
| Mileage cap | 100,000 miles, whichever comes first, both coverages[^1][^2][^8] |
| Capacity floor | 70% of original battery capacity[^2][^8] |
| Repair standard | Repaired or replaced to an EV Battery capacity of at least 70% of original, not to an as-new 100%; the replacement pack may be a new or a remanufactured Lithium-Ion Polymer battery[^2][^3][^8] |
| Transfers to a second owner | Yes. Any remaining portion of any warranty is fully transferable to subsequent owners except the 120 months/100,000 miles Power Train (Original Owner) warranty[^4][^5] |
| Clock starts | Date of First Service: the earliest of first retail delivery, lease, or entry into service as a company vehicle such as a demonstrator, rental or fleet vehicle[^6] |
| Outside the EV System Warranty | Other electrical components, including the traditional 12 volt battery, alternator and starter components[^1] |

## What is not covered

Kia excludes EV System and Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery damage or failure caused by:

- Ambient temperatures above 122F (50C) for over 24 hours, or storage below -22F (-30C) for over seven days.[^7]
- Leaving the vehicle over 14 days where the battery reaches a zero or near zero state of charge.[^7]
- Physically damaging the battery, intentionally attempting to reduce its life, exposing it to a direct flame, or immersing any portion of it in water or fluids.[^7]
- Opening the battery enclosure, or having it serviced by someone other than a Kia certified EV technician.[^7]
- Neglecting to follow correct charging procedures, or use of incompatible charging devices.[^7]
- Damage to the charge port or vehicle components caused by a public charger, a non-Kia supplied charge cord, or other third party EVSE; and incidental or consequential damage caused by the failure to repair an existing problem.[^7]

## Scope note

Kia writes one Warranty and Consumer Information Manual per model year for its electric vehicles rather than one per model, and the 2024 electric-vehicle edition is the one read here; nothing in it names the EV9. The 2025 and 2026 electric-vehicle editions are not on Kia's own hosts; Kia's undated US warranty page states the same ten-year, 100,000-mile terms and 70% floor for those years, and the exclusions and transfer terms above are the 2024 edition's. The manual delivered with a specific car is the authority for that car.
