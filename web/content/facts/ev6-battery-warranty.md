# Kia EV6: Battery Warranty

## Coverage at a glance

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is covered | Two warranties: the EV System Warranty (electric motor, high voltage battery, Electric Power Control Unit, On Board Charger) and Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery Capacity Coverage[^1][^2] |
| Term | 120 months (10 years), both coverages[^1][^2] |
| Mileage cap | 100,000 miles, whichever comes first, both coverages[^1][^2] |
| Capacity floor | 70% of original battery capacity[^2] |
| Repair standard | Repaired or replaced to at least 70% of original capacity, not to as-new; replacement pack may be new or remanufactured[^2][^3] |
| Transfers to a second owner | Yes, fully transferable. Only the 120 months/100,000 miles Power Train warranty is original-owner-only[^1][^4][^5] |
| Clock starts | Date of First Service: the earliest of first retail delivery, lease, or entry into service as a demonstrator, rental or fleet vehicle[^6] |
| Outside the EV System Warranty | Other electrical components, including the 12 volt battery, alternator and starter components[^1] |

- An EV6 that spent its first months as a dealer demonstrator or a rental started its ten years then, not when it was first sold to a customer.[^6]

## What is not covered

Kia excludes EV System and Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery damage or failure caused by:

- Ambient temperatures above 122F (50C) for over 24 hours, or storage below -22F (-30C) for over seven days.[^7]
- Leaving the vehicle over 14 days with the battery at or near a zero state of charge.[^7]
- Physically damaging the battery, intentionally reducing its life, exposing it to direct flame, or immersing any part of it in water or fluids.[^7]
- Opening the battery enclosure, or service by anyone other than a Kia certified EV technician.[^7]
- Incorrect charging procedures, or incompatible charging devices.[^7]
- A public charger, a non-Kia charge cord, or other third-party EVSE; and incidental or consequential damage from failing to repair an existing problem.[^7]

## Scope note

Kia writes one warranty manual per model year for its electric vehicles rather than one per model, and the 2024 edition is the one read here; nothing above names the EV6 specifically. Model years 2022, 2023, 2025 and 2026 were not checked. The manual delivered with a specific car is the authority for that car.
