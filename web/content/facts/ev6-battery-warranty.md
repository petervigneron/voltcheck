# Kia EV6: EV System Warranty and Battery Coverage

## Two separate coverages, both 10 years or 100,000 miles

Kia splits the electric drivetrain into two warranties, and they answer different questions.

- EV System Warranty. Kia's manual: "The EV System Warranty coverage period is 120 months or 100,000 miles from the Date of First Service, whichever comes first, and applies to the following EV System components: Electric Motor, High voltage Battery, Electric Power Control Unit (EPCU), On Board Charger (OBC)."[^1] This is the one that pays for a broken part.
- What it does not reach, in Kia's own words: "The EV System Warranty does not cover any other electrical components in the vehicle, such as (but not limited to) the traditional 12 volt car battery, alternator, or other starter components."[^1]
- Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery Capacity Coverage. Kia's manual: the coverage period "is 10 years or 100,000 miles from the Date of First Service, whichever comes first, for capacity loss below 70% of the original battery capacity."[^2] This is the one that pays for a pack that still works but has faded.
- Kia's own website states the same two periods, and describes the EV System Warranty as covering "the electric motor, high voltage battery, electric power control unit (EPCU), and on-board charger (OBC), for 10 years or 100,000 miles from the date of first service."[^3]

## What a capacity claim actually gets you

- The target is 70%, not new. Kia: "This warranty covers repairs needed to return battery capacity to 70% of original battery capacity."[^2]
- Kia says the quiet part in its own manual: a repair under this coverage "may not return your Lithium-Ion Battery to an 'as new' condition with the original 100% battery capacity. However, it will provide the vehicle with an EV Battery capacity of at least 70% of the original battery capacity."[^4]
- Repair comes before replacement: "If possible, the EV battery components will be repaired or replaced, and the original EV Battery will be returned to the vehicle. If necessary, the EV Battery will be replaced with either a new or remanufactured Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery."[^2]
- The coverage is not unconditional: it "is subject to the exclusions listed under the section 'What is Not Covered.'"[^4]

## The clock starts before you bought it

- Both coverages run from the Date of First Service.[^1][^2]
- Kia's manual defines that term: "'Date of First Service' means the first date the Kia Vehicle is delivered to the first retail purchaser, is leased or is placed into service as a company vehicle use (e.g., as a demonstrator, rental or fleet vehicle), whichever is earliest."[^5]
- An EV6 that spent its first months as a dealer demonstrator or a rental started its ten years then, not when it was first sold to a customer.

## It transfers — unlike the powertrain warranty

- Kia's manual states one rule for all of them: "Any remaining portion of any warranty, except the 120 months/100,000 miles Power Train (Original Owner) warranty, is fully transferable to subsequent owners."[^1]
- Kia's coverage chart shows the same split visually. The Power Train row carries an "Original Owner Only" band across its second half and a footnote reading "Original owner 120 Months/100,000 Miles Second and Subsequent Owner(s) 60 Months/60,000 Miles." The Electric Vehicle (EV) System row shows 120 months and 100,000 miles with no such band and no such footnote.[^6]
- Kia's website restricts only the powertrain coverage the same way, calling the 10-year/100,000-mile Powertrain Limited Warranty "applicable to the original purchaser and purchaser of a Certified Pre-Owned Kia," and attaching no equivalent condition to the EV System Warranty or EV Battery Coverage entries.[^3]
- Who counts as the original owner, and one lease case: "An Original Owner is defined as the first retail purchaser or lessee of the Kia Vehicle who takes delivery of the Kia Vehicle on its Date of First Service. If the Kia Vehicle was first placed in service as a lease vehicle, and the lessee purchases the vehicle at the end of the lease, the 120 months/100,000 miles Power Train Limited Warranty remains in effect."[^1]

## What is not covered

Kia lists these under "EV System and Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery" damage or failures resulting from or caused by:[^7]

- Exposing a vehicle to ambient temperatures above 122F (50C) for over 24 hours.
- Storing a vehicle in temperatures below -22F (-30C) for over seven days.
- Leaving the vehicle for over 14 days where the Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery reaches a zero or near zero state of charge.
- Physically damaging or intentionally attempting to reduce the life of the Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery.
- Exposing the Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery to contact with a direct flame.
- Immersing any portion of the Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery in water or fluids.
- Opening the Lithium-Ion Polymer Battery enclosure, or having it serviced by someone other than a Kia certified EV technician.
- Neglecting to follow correct charging procedures.
- Use of incompatible charging devices.
- "Damage to the charge port and/or vehicle components caused by a public charger, non-Kia supplied charge cord, or other third party electric vehicle service equipment (EVSE)."
- Incidental or consequential damage caused by the failure to repair an existing problem.

That last group is worth reading twice before buying a home charger or a travel adapter: Kia excludes damage caused by third-party EVSE by name.

## Which document governs

- Kia's website states the coverage but does not print the terms; it refers owners to their Warranty and Consumer Information Manual.
- The manual quoted throughout this page is Kia's own *2024 Warranty and Consumer Information Manual*, the electric-vehicle edition — Kia-branded, publication number UM 170 PS 001, printed June 20, 2023, 99 pages, with its EV coverage set out under the New Vehicle Limited Warranty.

## Scope note

Kia writes one warranty manual per model year for its electric vehicles rather than one per model, and the 2024 edition is the one opened here. Nothing quoted above names the EV6 specifically; it is the coverage Kia states for the electric vehicles that edition covers, and the EV6 has been sold in the US since model year 2022.

Model years 2022, 2023, 2025 and 2026 were not checked. Kia's website states the same 10-year/100,000-mile periods today, but the terms and exclusions above are the 2024 edition's wording, and the manual delivered with a specific car is the authority for that car.
