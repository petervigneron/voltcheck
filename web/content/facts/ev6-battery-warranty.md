# Kia EV6: EV System Warranty and Battery Coverage

## Two separate coverages, both 10 years or 100,000 miles

Kia splits the electric drivetrain into two warranties, and they answer different questions.

- EV System Warranty. Kia's words: it "safeguards key components of your electric drivetrain, including the electric motor, high voltage battery, electric power control unit (EPCU), and on-board charger (OBC), for 10 years or 100,000 miles from the date of first service, whichever comes first."[^1] This is the one that pays for a broken part.
- EV Battery Coverage. Kia's words: it covers "capacity loss below 70% of the original capacity for 10 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first."[^1] This is the one that pays for a pack that still works but has faded.
- What a claim gets you, in Kia's words: "Repairs or replacements restore the battery to at least 70% capacity."[^1]

## The clock starts before you bought it

- Both coverages run "from the date of first service."[^1] Kia's warranty manual defines that term: "'Date of First Service' means the first date the Kia Vehicle is delivered to the first retail purchaser, is leased or is placed into service as a company vehicle use (e.g., as a demonstrator, rental or fleet vehicle), whichever is earliest."[^2]
- A demonstrator or rental EV6 therefore started its ten years when the dealer put it into service, not when it was sold to a customer.

## Transfer

- Kia's warranty manual states one general rule: "Any remaining portion of any warranty, except the 120 month/100,000 mile Power Train (Original Owner) warranty, is fully transferable to subsequent owners."[^2]
- Kia's own page treats the powertrain warranty the same way, describing the 10-year/100,000-mile Powertrain Limited Warranty as "applicable to the original purchaser and purchaser of a Certified Pre-Owned Kia," and attaching no equivalent restriction to the EV System Warranty or EV Battery Coverage entries.[^1]
- The manual quoted above is Kia's 2020 US Warranty and Consumer Information Manual, which predates the EV6. It is quoted here as the wording of Kia's general transfer rule, not as a document that names the EV6. Kia's current US warranty manuals are behind an owner login, and no EV6-model-year US manual was opened for this sheet.

## What this page does not establish

- No model-year-specific US warranty manual for the EV6 was available without an owner login, so nothing here is sourced to a document that names the EV6 or its model year. The manual delivered with a specific car is the authority for that car.
- Kia's page states no exclusion list for the EV System Warranty or EV Battery Coverage. Exclusions certainly exist — they are in the manual — but this sheet does not guess at them.
- Kia's page states no measurement method for the 70% figure, and none is claimed here.

## Scope note

The coverage terms above come from Kia's own current US warranty page, which states them for Kia's electric vehicles as a group rather than per model. The EV6 has been sold in the US since model year 2022.

The transfer rule and the definition of "date of first service" come from Kia's 2020 US Warranty and Consumer Information Manual, the most recent full US manual reachable without an owner login. That document predates the EV6 and does not mention it. It is cited for the wording of Kia's general rules only, and this sheet says so in the section that uses it rather than leaving the reader to assume model-year coverage that was not confirmed.
