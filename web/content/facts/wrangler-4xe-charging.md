# Jeep Wrangler 4xe: Charging

## Pack

| Model year | Gross capacity, specification sheet | Pack capacity, press kit | Chemistry | Operating voltage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 17.3 kWh[^1] | 17 kWh[^5] | Lithium Ion Li-NMC[^1] | 260–400 VDC[^1] |
| 2023 | 17.3 kWh[^2] | | Lithium Ion Li-NMC[^2] | 260–400 VDC[^2] |
| 2024 | 17.3 kWh[^3] | 17 kWh[^13] | Lithium Ion Li-NMC[^3] | 260–400 VDC[^3] |
| 2025 | 17.3 kWh[^4] | 17 kWh[^14] | Lithium Ion Li-NMC[^4] | 260–400 VDC[^4] |

- The pack is a 96-cell, 400-volt unit mounted under the second-row seat, whose bottom cushion flips forward for access.[^5]

## Charge port

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| Charge inlet | SAE J1772, for AC Level 1 (120 V) and AC Level 2 (240 V)[^6][^7][^8][^9][^10] |
| Port location | Left front cowl, with LED charging-status indicators in the port[^5] |
| Maximum charge rate | 7.2 kW[^1][^2][^3][^4] |
| DC fast charging | Not offered[^1][^2][^3][^4][^6][^10] |

## Charge times

| Model years | Type of EVSE | Estimated charge time from a state of charge below 1% |
|---|---|---|
| 2021, 2022, 2024, 2025, 2026 | Level 1 (120 V/15 A) | Approximately 12 hours[^6][^7][^8][^9][^10] |
| 2021, 2022, 2024, 2025, 2026 | Level 2 (240 V/30 A or 32 A) | Approximately 2 hours[^6][^7][^8][^9][^10] |

- Jeep adds that a charge takes longer if a thermal self-protection reduces the current the EVSE delivers, and that with the ignition in ACC or RUN the charge indicator may stop at 99% while charging continues.[^6][^7][^8][^9][^10]

## Charging equipment

| Model years | Included with the vehicle | Level 2 charger Jeep recommends for home installation |
|---|---|---|
| 2021, 2022, 2024 | 120 Volt AC, SAE J1772 Level 1 EVSE — the Portable Charging Cordset — stored in the rear cargo area below the load floor[^6][^7][^8] | A 40 Amp Level 2 EVSE[^6][^7][^8] |
| 2025, 2026 | 120 Volt AC, SAE J1772 Level 1 EVSE — the Portable Charging Cordset — stored in the rear cargo area below the load floor[^9][^10] | A Level 2 EVSE charger with up to 48 amps[^9][^10] |

- Level 1 charging needs a conventional NEMA 5-15R 120 Volt AC grounded wall outlet; the cordset's own AC plug is a NEMA 5-15P right-angle plug.[^6][^7][^8][^9][^10]
- On 2025 and 2026 trucks Jeep directs owners to use only UL-certified (UL 2594) charging equipment.[^9][^10]

## Charging behavior

| Model years | Item | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 2021, 2022 | e-Save, Battery Save | Maintains the high voltage state of charge at its current level[^6][^7] |
| 2024, 2025, 2026 | e-Save, Battery Save | Maintains the high voltage state of charge at its current level under most driving scenarios[^8][^9][^10] |
| 2021, 2022, 2024, 2025, 2026 | e-Save, Battery Charge | Uses additional power from the gas engine to increase the high voltage state of charge, up to 95% capacity[^6][^7][^8][^9][^10] |
| 2021, 2022, 2024, 2025, 2026 | Charge schedule override | Plug the connector in, unplug it, and plug it back in within 10 seconds[^6][^7][^8][^9][^10] |
| 2021, 2022, 2024, 2025, 2026 | Cold-weather conditioning | At 5°F (-15°C) or below at shut down, the instrument cluster displays "Plug In Vehicle To Condition Battery"[^6][^7][^10][^15][^16] |
| 2021, 2022, 2024, 2025 | Charging with the hood open | Disabled while the hood is open, and resumes when the hood closes[^6][^7][^8][^9] |

- Max Regeneration uses the regenerative braking system to slow the truck when the driver lifts off the accelerator, and stays selected until the driver turns it off.[^6][^7][^10][^15][^16]
- With 4x4 engaged, all four wheels feed torque for regenerative braking; Jeep states the powertrain engages up to 0.25 g of regenerative braking on the brake pedal.[^5]
