# Nissan Leaf (model years 2018–2026): Heat Pump by Year

## What Nissan calls it matters here

Nissan's US brochures for the 2018 through 2025 Leaf never use the words "heat pump." They list a row called "Hybrid heater system" instead, and give it per trim.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6][^7][^8]

Nissan does use the words "heat pump," plainly, for the 2026 Leaf.[^9]

This page reports Nissan's own term for each model year and does not treat "Hybrid heater system" and "heat pump" as the same claim. No Nissan document opened for this sheet equates the two.

## Hybrid heater system, by model year and trim (2018–2025)

- 2018. Not offered on S. Part of the SV All-Weather Package on SV. Standard on SL.[^1]
- 2019. Not offered on S or S PLUS. Part of the SV/SV PLUS All-Weather Package on SV and SV PLUS. Standard on SL and SL PLUS.[^2]
- 2020. Not offered on S or S PLUS. Part of the SV/SV PLUS All-Weather Package on SV and SV PLUS. Standard on SL PLUS.[^3]
- 2021. Not offered on S or S PLUS. Standard on SV, SV PLUS, and SL PLUS. This is the year Nissan stopped gating it behind a package on the SV trims.[^4]
- 2022. Not offered on S or S PLUS. Standard on SV, SV PLUS, and SL PLUS.[^5]
- 2023. Nissan's lineup narrows to two trims. Not offered on S. Standard on SV PLUS.[^6]
- 2024. Not offered on S. Standard on SV PLUS.[^7]
- 2025. Not offered on S. Standard on SV PLUS.[^8]

For every one of these eight model years, a Leaf S has no Hybrid heater system, and Nissan's brochure leaves the cell blank rather than marking it optional.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6][^7][^8]

## 2026

The 2026 Leaf, a new generation, has a heat pump standard on every trim. Nissan's own words: "To further optimize energy usage during cold conditions, LEAF has a standard, energy-efficient heat pump to help warm the cabin."[^9]

Nissan describes a separate, optional battery heater on the same car. Nissan's own words: "An available battery heater further contributes to winter charging performance; the driver can engage the heater to warm the battery before charging in cold weather."[^9]

The 2026 Leaf also captures waste heat. Nissan's own words: "LEAF also now captures wasted heat from the drive motor and the on-board charger, and re-purposes that heat to help warm the battery to its ideal temperature range."[^9]

## Scope note

Model years 2018 through 2026, US market. Model years 2018 through 2025 were read off Nissan's own brochure specification grids, one per year, at the cell level: each trim's mark was read from a rendered image of the page rather than from extracted text, because the extracted text of these grids does not keep marks aligned to their columns.

Model years 2011 through 2017, the first-generation Leaf, are not covered. No brochure for those years was opened for this sheet.

The central limitation is stated in the first section rather than buried here: for 2018 through 2025, Nissan publishes a feature called "Hybrid heater system" and does not say in any document opened here what that system is, or whether it contains a heat pump. This page therefore does not answer "does the 2018-2025 Leaf have a heat pump" with a yes or a no. It reports which trims Nissan gives the Hybrid heater system to, and leaves the equivalence unclaimed.

The 2026 heat-pump claim is Nissan's own, in a Nissan press release, and covers the third-generation Leaf only. It should not be read backwards onto the 2018-2025 car.
