# Nissan Leaf (model years 2018–2026): Heat Pump by Year

## What Nissan calls it matters here

Nissan's US brochures for the 2018 through 2025 Leaf never use the words "heat pump." They list a row called "Hybrid heater system" instead, and give it per trim.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6][^7][^8]

For the 2026 Leaf — a new, third-generation car — Nissan does two things it never did for the older one. It uses the words "heat pump" plainly in prose,[^9] and in the press kit's specification grid it heads the row **"Hybrid heater system (heat pump)"**, glossing its own term.[^10]

So the equation exists, in Nissan's own hand, for the 2026 car. What Nissan has never said is whether it reaches backwards. No 2018-2025 Nissan document opened for this sheet equates the two terms, and the 2026 press kit does not say the gloss describes the older generation's hardware — it is a spec grid for one model year of one new car. This page therefore states the 2026 gloss where it belongs and does not carry it back to 2018-2025, where the row's contents remain unstated by Nissan.

One thing a reader may find and should not over-read: Nissan's US Quick Reference Guides for the Leaf do print the words "heat pump," in a cold-weather note that reads "Using the climate control system to heat the cabin when the outside temperature is below 32°F (0°C) uses more electricity (heat pump, if so equipped) and affects vehicle range more than using the heater at temperatures above 32°F (0°C)." That is conditional on its face — "if so equipped" — and it is shared boilerplate, not a Leaf statement: the identical sentence appears verbatim in Nissan's Ariya Quick Reference Guides for 2023, 2024 and 2025, and in the Leaf guides for 2023 and 2025. It tells a reader nothing about whether any particular Leaf has one.[^11][^12][^13]

## Hybrid heater system, by model year and trim (2018–2025)

- 2018. Not offered on S. Part of the SV All-Weather Package on SV. Standard on SL.[^1]
- 2019. Not offered on S or S PLUS. Part of the SV/SV PLUS All-Weather Package on SV and SV PLUS. Standard on SL and SL PLUS.[^2]
- 2020. Not offered on S or S PLUS. Part of the SV/SV PLUS All-Weather Package on SV and SV PLUS. Standard on SL PLUS.[^3]
- 2021. Not offered on S or S PLUS. Standard on SV, SV PLUS, and SL PLUS. This is the year Nissan stopped gating it behind a package on the SV trims.[^4]
- 2022. Not offered on S or S PLUS. Standard on SV, SV PLUS, and SL PLUS.[^5]
- 2023. Nissan's lineup narrows to two trims. Not offered on S. Standard on SV PLUS.[^6]
- 2024. Not offered on S. Standard on SV PLUS.[^7]
- 2025. Not offered on S. Standard on SV PLUS.[^8]

For every one of these eight model years, a Leaf S has no Hybrid heater system, and Nissan's brochure leaves the cell blank rather than marking it optional.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6][^7][^8]

## 2026

The 2026 Leaf, a new generation, has a heat pump — but **not on every trim**, and the base car does not get one.

Nissan's press release describes it as standard: "To further optimize energy usage during cold conditions, LEAF has a standard, energy-efficient heat pump to help warm the cabin."[^9] Nissan's own specification grid for the same car is more precise, and it is the grid that tells a shopper which car to buy. In the 2026 LEAF Press Kit's "Comfort and Convenience Features" table, the row "Hybrid heater system (heat pump)" reads:[^10]

- **S+ — "-"**. Not marked standard and not marked optional.
- **SV+ — "S"**, standard.
- **PLAT+ — "S"**, standard.

Nissan's legend for that grid defines two marks only: "**S** – Standard **O** – Optional." A dash is neither, and the row is absent from the press kit's list of factory-installed options, so on the S+ the heat pump is not something a buyer can add. Where the press release says "standard" without qualification and the grid gives the base trim a dash, this page reports the grid: it is the document that resolves per trim, and the difference costs a shopper the wrong car.[^9][^10]

The battery heater is separate, and Nissan prices it. The same grid marks the "Battery heater" row "-" on S+ and "O" on both SV+ and PLAT+, and the press kit's "FACTORY INSTALLED OPTIONS" table lists "Battery Heater" at **$300**, available on SV+ and Platinum+ and blank for S+. Nissan's prose is looser: "An available battery heater further contributes to winter charging performance; the driver can engage the heater to warm the battery before charging in cold weather."[^9][^10]

The 2026 Leaf also captures waste heat. Nissan's own words: "LEAF also now captures wasted heat from the drive motor and the on-board charger, and re-purposes that heat to help warm the battery to its ideal temperature range."[^9]

A 2026 S+ therefore has neither the heat pump nor the battery heater, on Nissan's own grid. That is the same shape the 2018-2025 car had, where the base S never got the Hybrid heater system either.

## Scope note

Model years 2018 through 2026, US market. Model years 2018 through 2025 were read off Nissan's own brochure specification grids, one per year, at the cell level: each trim's mark was read from a rendered image of the page rather than from extracted text, because the extracted text of these grids does not keep marks aligned to their columns.

Model years 2011 through 2017, the first-generation Leaf, are not covered. No brochure for those years was opened for this sheet.

The central limitation is stated in the first section rather than buried here: for 2018 through 2025, Nissan publishes a feature called "Hybrid heater system" and does not say in any document opened here what that system is, or whether it contains a heat pump. This page therefore does not answer "does the 2018-2025 Leaf have a heat pump" with a yes or a no. It reports which trims Nissan gives the Hybrid heater system to, and leaves the equivalence unclaimed.

Nissan does equate the two terms once, in the 2026 press kit's grid heading "Hybrid heater system (heat pump)". That is a document about the third-generation car and says nothing about what was in a 2018-2025 Leaf. Whether the gloss describes the older hardware as well is not something Nissan states anywhere, and this page does not infer it in either direction — the 2026 gloss is reported where Nissan puts it and carried no further.

Where Nissan's own two 2026 documents disagree, the more granular one is followed. The press release says the heat pump is "standard"; the press kit's per-trim grid gives the S+ a dash and marks only SV+ and PLAT+ standard. A press release describing a lineup and a spec grid resolving it per trim are not the same kind of claim, and the grid is the one a shopper can act on. Nothing here reads the press release as wrong — only as unscoped.

The 2026 grid rows were read from the press kit's table markup, in which each mark occupies its own cell bound by name to its row header. That is the same precaution the 2018-2025 brochure grids needed and got, for the same reason: in these Nissan tables the marks sit visually above their row labels, and any reading that flattens the table first will slide a mark onto the wrong row.
