# Nissan Leaf: Charging

## DC fast charging

| Model year and pack | Fast-charge port | Charger power Nissan states | Time to 80% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2019 and 2024, 40 kWh | CHAdeMO, factory option[^1][^2] | Up to 50 kW[^1][^2] | Approximately 40 minutes at the battery-temperature gauge's middle reading; longer when cold or hot[^1][^2] |
| 2019, 62 kWh and 2024, 60 kWh | CHAdeMO, factory option[^1][^2] | Up to 100 kW[^1][^2] | Approximately 60 minutes on a 50 kW charger, approximately 45 minutes on a 100 kW charger, at the gauge's middle reading; longer when cold or hot[^1][^2] |
| 2026, 75 kWh liquid-cooled | NACS, passenger-side fender[^3] | Up to 150 kW[^3] | 10% to 80% in 35 minutes[^3] |

Nissan's own words: "Quick charge capability is only available on vehicles manufactured with the quick charge option, which includes the quick charge port. If your vehicle does not have such a port, quick charging cannot be used."[^1][^2]

- Nothing in the model name or the trim says which Leaf has the port, so it is a per-car check.[^1][^2]
- The 100 kW figure carries Nissan's asterisk: subject to the charging equipment's capability to output 100 kW.[^1][^2]
- Quick-charge power is limited when the pack temperature is near the gauge's red zone.[^1][^2]

## Connector and adapters

| Model year | AC port | DC fast-charge port |
|---|---|---|
| 2019 and 2024 | SAE J1772[^1][^2] | CHAdeMO, factory option[^1][^2] |
| 2026 | J1772, driver's-side fender[^3] | NACS, passenger-side fender[^3] |

- A quick-charge-equipped Leaf works with most CHAdeMO connectors on charging stations.[^1][^2]
- Nissan's own caution in the manual: it supports CHAdeMO, but the connector may not become the US SAE standard.[^1][^2]
- The 2026 car's NACS port reaches over 20,000 Tesla Superchargers.[^3]
- Plug and Charge: the 2026 Leaf is the first Nissan with it, and it requires a subscription and activation through the MyNISSAN app.[^3]

## Home charging (Level 2)

| Method | Supply |
|---|---|
| Normal charge (Level 2) | AC 220-240 volt, 16 A or 32 A[^2] |
| Trickle charge (Level 1) | AC 110-120 volt, 15 A dedicated outlet[^2] |
| Onboard charger power in kW | Not published[^1][^2] |

- Nissan does not recommend trickle charging for regular use, because it takes more time than normal charging.[^2]

## Battery care

| Topic | What Nissan tells Leaf owners |
|---|---|
| Everyday charging | Use normal charging; minimize quick charge to help prolong Li-ion battery life[^2] |
| Extending battery life | Use normal or trickle charging and minimize public fast or quick charging[^4] |
| Highway trips | Avoid extended highway driving with multiple quick charges[^4] |
| Repeated quick charging | Charging may be terminated automatically, especially in extreme hot weather[^2] |

## Preconditioning and battery warmer

| Feature | Leaf |
|---|---|
| Warming the pack before a fast charge | No such feature is described in Nissan's 2019 or 2024 owner's manual[^1][^2] |
| Li-ion battery warmer, 40 kWh | On at approximately -1°F (-17°C) battery or colder, off at approximately 14°F (-10°C)[^5] |
| Li-ion battery warmer, 60 kWh | On at approximately -4°F (-20°C) battery or colder with outside temperature approximately -11°F (-24°C) or colder, off at approximately 0°F (-18°C) battery or -8°F (-22°C) outside[^6] |

- 40 kWh: the warmer draws from the pack when the car is unplugged, and does not operate below approximately 15% charge unless the charger is connected.[^5]
- 60 kWh: the warmer does not operate unless the normal charger is connected.[^6]

## Scope note

Model years 2019 and 2024 were read directly from Nissan's own owner's manuals; 2011 through 2018, 2020 through 2023 and 2025 were not checked and are not claimed. Every 2026 figure comes from Nissan's June 17, 2025 press release for the third-generation Leaf, and none of it is applied to an earlier car. Nissan's times to 80% are keyed to the battery-temperature gauge, and the figures above are the gauge's middle reading, which is the best case.
