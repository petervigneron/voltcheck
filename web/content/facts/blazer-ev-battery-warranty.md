# Chevrolet Blazer EV: Battery Warranty

## Coverage at a glance

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is covered | The propulsion battery pack and its internal components[^1][^2][^3][^4] |
| Term | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6][^7] |
| Capacity floor | The battery is replaced or repaired if capacity falls below 75% of its original value during the warranty period, as determined by a certified dealer[^1][^2][^3][^4] |
| What you get back | A battery appropriate for the age and mileage of the vehicle — repaired or replaced with new or refurbished components, at Chevrolet's discretion[^1][^3][^4] |
| Transfers to a second owner | Yes. The warranty is provided to the original owner and any subsequent owners of the vehicle during the warranty period[^1][^3][^4]. On 2024 cars it transfers at no cost to any subsequent person who assumes ownership within the term, with no deductibles[^2] |
| Clock starts | The date the vehicle is first delivered or put in use[^1][^2][^3][^4]. The 2024 booklet words the battery term as running from the original in-service date[^2] |
| Cost of a covered repair | No charge, including towing, parts and labor[^1][^2] |
| Where the work is done | An authorized Chevrolet dealership, from a network of certified dealers trained on EV battery packs[^1][^3][^4]. On 2024 cars, an EV Certified Chevrolet dealer facility[^2] |
| Bumper-to-bumper coverage | 3 years or 36,000 miles, whichever comes first[^1][^5] |

## Roadside assistance

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| Towing | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first[^14] |
| Non-tow services | 5 years or 60,000 miles, whichever comes first[^14] |
| Its relation to the warranty | A separate Chevrolet program, outside the coverage the New Vehicle Limited Warranty provides, and Chevrolet may change or discontinue it without notification[^14] |

## Uses of the battery that stay covered

| Use | Answer |
|---|---|
| Backup power during an outage | Covered as normal use[^1][^3][^4] |
| Selling energy back to the electrical grid | Covered as reasonable use; additional vehicle and in-home hardware may be required[^1][^3][^4] |
| Mobile power source — job-site power, vehicle-to-vehicle charging | Covered as reasonable use[^1][^3][^4] |

## California and the states that follow it

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| High Voltage Battery Warranty | 8 years or 100,000 miles, whichever comes first[^8] |
| State-of-health floor | GM warrants the battery free from defects in materials and workmanship that cause its state of health to deteriorate to less than 70% over that period[^9] |
| Propulsion-related short-term defects | 3 years or 50,000 miles, whichever comes first[^8] |
| Propulsion-related long-term defects | 7 years or 70,000 miles, whichever comes first[^8] |
| Parts covered beyond the standard warranty period | The entries GM marks with an asterisk on its California Propulsion Related Parts Warranty List — among them the battery energy control module, high voltage cables, front drive unit, rear drive unit, drive motor power inverter, battery charger coupler and battery charger[^9] |
| Who it reaches | Vehicles certified for sale in California and serviced by an authorized Chevrolet or GM dealership in California, Massachusetts, New York, Oregon, Vermont or Washington[^9] |
| Vehicle weight limit | Under 10,000 lb GVWR[^8] |

## What is not covered

Chevrolet excludes from the New Vehicle Limited Warranty:

- On 2025 and later cars, damage to the battery from incompatible charging methods, or from use of an adapter that General Motors does not sell or approve.[^10]
- On 2025 and later cars, excessive use of the main battery for non-propulsion purposes, including semi-permanent or permanent daily power provisions. GM reserves the right to curtail non-propulsion use it determines is harmful to the battery.[^11]
- Alteration, modification or tampering with the vehicle after final assembly, including its software; installation of non-GM parts; water or fluid contamination.[^2][^12]
- Collision, fire, theft, freezing, vandalism, riot, explosion, or objects striking the vehicle.[^2][^12]
- Damage from failure to follow the recommended maintenance schedule intervals.[^2][^13]
- Any vehicle whose odometer has been disconnected or altered, or whose mileage cannot be determined; the warranty is void on vehicles currently or previously titled as salvaged, scrapped, junked or otherwise considered a total loss.[^2][^12]
- Economic loss and extra expense, including inconvenience, lodging and meals, loss of vehicle use, loss of time or pay, and storage.[^2][^10]

## Scope note

Chevrolet writes one electric-vehicle warranty booklet per model year covering its whole EV line rather than one per model, and the 2024, 2025, 2026 and 2027 editions are the ones read here; the Blazer EV is not named in any of them, and the model-specific 8-year/100,000-mile term comes from Chevrolet's own Blazer EV specification pages. The California ZEV section appears in the 2026 edition, and the two exclusions marked 2025 and later were added after the 2024 edition. The booklet delivered with a specific car is the authority for that car.
