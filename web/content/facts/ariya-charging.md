# Nissan Ariya (2024 model year): Charging

## DC fast charging

- Peak DC fast-charge rate: 130 kW, on every Ariya trim, 63 kWh and 87 kWh packs alike.[^1]
- Time to charge from a low-battery warning to 80% on a DC fast charger:
  - 35 minutes on the 63 kWh battery: Engage FWD, Engage Dual Motor AWD[^2]
  - 40 minutes on the 87 kWh battery: Venture+, Engage+ Dual Motor AWD, Evolve+, Evolve+ Dual Motor AWD, Empower+, Platinum+ Dual Motor AWD[^2]
- A charger rated above 130 kW will not charge the Ariya any faster: Nissan caps input at 130 kW regardless of the charger's own rating.[^3]
- Est. A State Of Charge test of a 2025 Platinum+ e-4ORCE (87 kWh usable) on a 325 kW EVgo charger reached 130 kW peak and averaged 110 kW from 10% to 80%, finishing in 31.5 minutes. That is faster than Nissan's own 40-minute estimate for that battery.[^4]

## Connector

- The Ariya's built-in DC fast-charge port is CCS1 (Combined Charging System). It does not have a native NACS (Tesla-style) port.[^3]
- A Nissan-compliant NACS adapter is required to charge at a Tesla Supercharger. MSRP $235, sold by Nissan dealers and NissanUSA.com. Nissan states any other adapter is "strictly prohibited" for use with the Ariya.[^5]
- The adapter does not slow charging: Nissan states it delivers "approximately the same charging speeds" as the native CCS1 port.[^5]
- Tesla Level 2 Destination Chargers, Wall Connectors, and Mobile Connectors are not compatible with the Ariya, with or without the adapter.[^5]
- The adapter only works at Tesla Superchargers Nissan calls V3 and V4 stalls. Nissan's own words: "Ariya is compatible with certain Tesla Superchargers (V3 and V4 models)." Older Supercharger stalls are not included.[^5]
- Nissan said in July 2023 that it planned to sell "EVs for the U.S. and Canadian markets with a NACS port" starting in 2025, without naming which vehicles, and added that "more details on the rollout... will be communicated at a later date." Nissan's most recent statement found since then (the Feb 13, 2025 release cited above) still describes the Ariya's port as CCS1 with an adapter, not a native NACS port. Everything in this Connector section is accurate as of that Feb 13, 2025 date; it should be reconfirmed against a later Nissan source before being applied to an Ariya built after that date.[^6]

## Preconditioning

- The Ariya warms its battery before fast charging through a driver-activated feature called "Battery Heater." It is manual: it does not trigger automatically from route guidance to a charger.[^7]
- To turn it on while driving, Nissan's manual gives these steps: place the power switch in READY to drive (the setting can't be made while driving), touch the house-shaped icon on the Launch bar, then touch the gear-shaped icon and touch "EV," then touch "Battery Heater" and touch "ON."[^8]
- Nissan's guidance: turn Battery Heater on 30 minutes to 1 hour before arriving at a DC fast charger when a low-temperature alert appears on the dash.[^3]
- Battery Heater switches off automatically once fast charging finishes, or when the vehicle is powered off.[^9]

## AC (Level 2, home) charging

- Onboard AC charger: 7.2 kW, standard on every US Ariya trim.[^1]

## Scope note

Every non-"est." figure in DC fast charging, Preconditioning, and AC charging above is sourced to Nissan's own 2024-model-year brochure and owner's manual. It has not been checked against the 2023 or 2025 model year's brochure/owner's manual, so this sheet should not be presented as applying to those years without that separate check.

The Connector section is dated more precisely, and carries its own caveat inline: it is accurate as of Nissan's Feb 13, 2025 press release, the most recent Nissan statement found describing the Ariya's port. Nissan has separately said (July 2023) that it planned to sell some U.S./Canada EVs with a native NACS port "starting in 2025," without naming which vehicles; see the Connector section for exactly what is, and isn't, resolved by Nissan's own material on whether that applies to the Ariya.
