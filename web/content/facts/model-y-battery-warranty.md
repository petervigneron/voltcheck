# Tesla Model Y: Battery and Drive Unit Limited Warranty

## How long it lasts, in Tesla's two vocabularies

Tesla publishes the same coverage twice, and the two documents name trims differently. Both are quoted here so you can find whichever name is on your car's paperwork.

- Tesla's warranty document, effective June 5, 2025, states coverage by battery family:[^1]
  - "Model 3 and Model Y Standard or Standard Range Plus - 8 years or 100,000 miles (160,000 km), whichever comes first, with minimum 70% retention of Battery capacity over the warranty period."
  - "Model 3 and Model Y Long Range or Performance - 8 years or 120,000 miles (192,000 km), whichever comes first, with minimum 70% retention of Battery capacity over the warranty period."
- Tesla's support page states coverage by trim name:[^2]
  - 8 years or 100,000 miles, minimum 70% retention: Model Y RWD, Model Y AWD.
  - 8 years or 120,000 miles, minimum 70% retention: Model Y Premium RWD, Model Y Premium AWD, Model Y Performance AWD, Model Y L Premium Launch Series.
- The two vocabularies do not overlap. The words "AWD" and "Premium" do not appear anywhere in the 18 pages of Tesla's warranty document, and the support page's table names Model Y trims only as RWD, AWD, Premium and Performance variants.

## Finding your car in the right list

- If the name on your paperwork appears in one of these lists, read that list.
- If it appears in neither — an older Model Y sold under a name Tesla no longer uses is the common case — this sheet does not tell you which row it maps to. Matching on the drivetrain letters alone is not safe: "AWD" appears in the support page's 100,000-mile row and in its 120,000-mile row, attached to different trim names.
- Two Tesla-stated facts decide it instead of a guess. First, Tesla's rule about which document governs: "The limited warranties above apply only to vehicles purchased directly from Tesla on or after the date listed on the limited warranty; any Tesla vehicles purchased prior to that date are subject to the applicable limited warranty effective as of the date of purchase of such vehicle directly from Tesla."[^2] An older Model Y is under an older document, not this one. Second, Tesla's instruction: "This warranty should only be used for general knowledge. For the authoritative information of your vehicle, view the warranty provided with your vehicle."[^2]
- Tesla also states where to read it per-car: the Tesla app's 'Specs & Warranty' screen has a 'Warranty' tab showing the coverage for that vehicle.[^2]

## What Tesla means by 70% retention

- The Battery and Drive Unit Limited Warranty states the floor in one phrase and no more: "minimum 70% retention of Battery capacity over the warranty period."[^1] It does not define how that retention is measured or what evidence establishes a claim.
- Ordinary degradation is excluded, in the same section. Tesla: "The Battery, like all lithium-ion batteries, will experience gradual energy or power loss with time and use. Loss of Battery energy or power over time or due to or resulting from Battery usage is NOT covered under this Battery and Drive Unit Limited Warranty, except to the extent specified in this Battery and Drive Unit Limited Warranty."[^4]
- The number on your dash is not the measurement. Tesla: "Note that the vehicle's range estimates are an imperfect measure of Battery capacity because they are affected by additional factors separate from Battery capacity."[^1]
- A warranty replacement need not be a new pack: "the replacement Battery will be in a condition appropriate to the age and mileage of the vehicle sufficient to achieve or exceed the minimum Battery capacity for the remainder of the warranty period of the original Battery."[^1]
- Software updates are carved out: "Any noticeable changes to the performance of the Battery due to these software updates are NOT covered."[^4]
- Tesla does phrase the 70% floor as a defect standard — "free from defects in material and workmanship that would cause the retention of Battery capacity to fall below 70%" — but that is the Battery ZEV Limited Warranty's wording,[^3] which Tesla limits to model year 2026 and later. It is not the Battery and Drive Unit Limited Warranty's wording, and it does not describe the coverage on a pre-2026 Model Y.

## It transfers to you

- Tesla: "This New Vehicle Limited Warranty is transferable at no cost to any person(s) who subsequently and lawfully assume(s) ownership of the vehicle after the first retail purchaser within the described limitations of this New Vehicle Limited Warranty."[^5]
- Tesla's support page adds the condition: it transfers "when a vehicle ownership transfer is performed through Tesla."[^2]

## The ZEV warranties, and the two conditions that gate them

- These are separate coverages, not part of the Battery and Drive Unit Limited Warranty.
- Model year: "NOTE: The ZEV Limited Warranties apply only to vehicles of Model Year 2026 and later."[^3]
- Warranty Region: Tesla "provides four ZEV Limited Warranties, which apply to all vehicles with a Warranty Region of the United States and Canada, except as specifically noted below."[^3] Tesla's support page puts the same condition as "If your New Vehicle Limited Warranty is valid in the United States or Canada, your vehicle may be covered by the ZEV Limited Warranties."[^2]
- Battery ZEV Limited Warranty: 8 years or 100,000 miles (160,000 km), whichever comes first.[^3]
- High-Priced Propulsion-Related Parts ZEV Limited Warranty: 7 years or 70,000 miles (110,000 km), whichever comes first.[^3]

## What is excluded

- Tesla excludes damage from "intentional actions (including intentionally abusing or destroying your vehicle or ignoring active vehicle warnings or service notifications), a collision or accident (excluding from Battery fires as specified above), or the servicing or opening of the Battery or Drive Unit by non-Tesla or non-certified personnel."[^4]
- Also excluded: attempting to extend or reduce the Battery's life by physical means or programming, exposing the Battery to direct flame, and flooding the Battery.[^4]
- Battery fire runs the other way, with a carve-back. Tesla: this warranty "will also cover damage to your vehicle from a Battery fire even if it is the result of driver error." Tesla's next sentence limits that: "(Coverage will not extend to damage that had already been sustained before a Battery fire occurred, or to any damage if the Battery fire occurred after your vehicle had already been totaled.)"[^1]
- Being excluded is not the same as the warranty being void. Tesla has a separate "Voided Warranty" section listing the circumstances in which the whole New Vehicle Limited Warranty can be voided for a car — among them a defaced VIN, a tampered odometer, a branded or unclean title, and a vehicle an insurer has declared a total loss.[^6]

## Scope note

Both sources are the ones Tesla publishes today: a warranty document whose cover page gives an effective date of 2025/06/05, and Tesla's current support page. Neither states a model year for the Model Y coverage tiers.

This sheet does not map Tesla's older trim names onto its current ones. No Tesla document opened for this sheet states that mapping, and inventing one would decide a 20,000-mile difference in coverage on a guess. Where a car's name is not in either list above, the page sends the reader to the booklet delivered with that car and to Tesla, which is also Tesla's own instruction.
