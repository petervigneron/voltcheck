# Tesla Model Y: Battery and Drive Unit Limited Warranty

## Two Tesla sources, and they do not line up on the Model Y AWD

- Tesla's warranty document, effective June 5, 2025, splits the Model Y by battery family:[^1]
  - "Model 3 and Model Y Standard or Standard Range Plus - 8 years or 100,000 miles (160,000 km), whichever comes first, with minimum 70% retention of Battery capacity over the warranty period."
  - "Model 3 and Model Y Long Range or Performance - 8 years or 120,000 miles (192,000 km), whichever comes first, with minimum 70% retention of Battery capacity over the warranty period."
- Tesla's support page splits it by current trim name, and puts an all-wheel-drive Model Y in the 100,000-mile tier:[^2]
  - Model Y RWD and Model Y AWD: 8 years or 100,000 miles, minimum 70% retention.
  - Model Y Premium RWD, Model Y Premium AWD, Model Y Performance AWD, Model Y L Premium Launch Series: 8 years or 120,000 miles, minimum 70% retention.
- These two lists are not interchangeable. A car sold as a Model Y Long Range AWD sits in Tesla's 120,000-mile bullet in the warranty document, while Tesla's support page lists "Model Y AWD" at 100,000 miles. This sheet states both, because both are Tesla's, and does not resolve them. The warranty booklet delivered with a specific car is the authority for that car.
- The Model 3's two lists do not have this mismatch. Every Model 3 all-wheel-drive trim on Tesla's support page — Premium AWD and Performance AWD — sits at 120,000 miles.[^2]

## What Tesla means by 70% retention

- It is a defect standard. Tesla's Battery ZEV wording states it directly: the Battery will "be free from defects in material and workmanship that would cause the retention of Battery capacity to fall below 70%."[^3]
- Ordinary degradation is excluded. Tesla: "The Battery, like all lithium-ion batteries, will experience gradual energy or power loss with time and use. Loss of Battery energy or power over time or due to or resulting from Battery usage is NOT covered under this Battery and Drive Unit Limited Warranty, except to the extent specified in this Battery and Drive Unit Limited Warranty."[^4]
- The dash number is not the measurement. Tesla: "Note that the vehicle's range estimates are an imperfect measure of Battery capacity because they are affected by additional factors separate from Battery capacity."[^1]
- A warranty replacement need not be a new pack: "the replacement Battery will be in a condition appropriate to the age and mileage of the vehicle sufficient to achieve or exceed the minimum Battery capacity for the remainder of the warranty period of the original Battery."[^1]
- Software updates are carved out: "Any noticeable changes to the performance of the Battery due to these software updates are NOT covered."[^4]

## It transfers to you

- Tesla: "This New Vehicle Limited Warranty is transferable at no cost to any person(s) who subsequently and lawfully assume(s) ownership of the vehicle after the first retail purchaser within the described limitations of this New Vehicle Limited Warranty."[^5]
- Tesla's support page adds the condition: it transfers "when a vehicle ownership transfer is performed through Tesla."[^2]

## The ZEV warranties, and the model year that gates them

- Tesla limits these by model year: "NOTE: The ZEV Limited Warranties apply only to vehicles of Model Year 2026 and later."[^3]
- Battery ZEV Limited Warranty: 8 years or 100,000 miles (160,000 km), whichever comes first.[^3]
- High-Priced Propulsion-Related Parts ZEV Limited Warranty: 7 years or 70,000 miles (110,000 km), whichever comes first.[^3]

## What voids it

- Tesla excludes damage from "intentional actions (including intentionally abusing or destroying your vehicle or ignoring active vehicle warnings or service notifications), a collision or accident (excluding from Battery fires as specified above), or the servicing or opening of the Battery or Drive Unit by non-Tesla or non-certified personnel."[^4]
- Also excluded: attempting to extend or reduce the Battery's life by physical means or programming, exposing the Battery to direct flame, and flooding the Battery.[^4]
- Battery fires run the other way: this warranty "will also cover damage to your vehicle from a Battery fire even if it is the result of driver error."[^1]

## Scope note

Both sources are the ones Tesla publishes today: a warranty document whose cover page gives an effective date of 2025/06/05, and Tesla's current support page. Neither states a model year for the Model Y coverage tiers, and this sheet does not claim what an older Model Y's warranty says.

Tesla itself signals that older cars can differ: it attaches a sentence to its Model S and Model X bullet stating that a car "purchased prior to the effective date specified on the cover page of this New Vehicle Limited Warranty is subject to the applicable Battery and Drive Unit Warranty effective as of the date of purchase." No such sentence appears on the Model 3 and Model Y bullet. Tesla's own instruction on the support page: "This warranty should only be used for general knowledge. For the authoritative information of your vehicle, view the warranty provided with your vehicle."
