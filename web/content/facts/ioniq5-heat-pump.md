# Hyundai Ioniq 5 (model years 2022–2026, US market): Heat Pump

## The answer is the drivetrain, not the trim

Hyundai's own equipment grid gives the Heat Pump row the same value in every trim column, every model year from 2022 through 2025: **AWD only**. It is not an option a buyer of a rear-wheel-drive Ioniq 5 could add, and it is not a reason to move up a trim.

- 2022, trims SE / SEL / Limited: "AWD only" in all three columns.[^1]
- 2023, trims SE / SEL / Limited: "AWD only" in all three columns.[^2]
- 2024, trims SE / SEL / Limited: "AWD-only" in all three columns.[^3]
- 2025, trims SE / SEL / XRT / Limited: "AWD-only" in all four columns.[^4]

Every rear-wheel-drive Ioniq 5 in those four model years is therefore a car without a heat pump, including every SE Standard Range, which Hyundai builds only as RWD.[^4]

The 2025 XRT is the one trim the rule resolves on its own: Hyundai lists an XRT range figure only in the AWD column of its 2025 range table, and XRT appears in neither the RWD column nor the RWD Standard Range column.[^4]

## What an RWD Ioniq 5 heats the cabin with instead

A PTC heater. Hyundai's grid carries a separate PTC Heater row directly above the Heat Pump row and marks it "S" — standard — in every trim column in 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

## Model year 2026

Hyundai's own 2026 Model Year Changes document lists the "2026 IONIQ 5 (EV)" as a **Carry-over Model**, and its table of changes from the '25 model year to the '26 names three things: the included charging cable (Level 1, changed to a Level 1 / Level 2 combo), a new Sage Silver Matte color, and two colors moving from XRT-only to all trims. No HVAC change is listed.[^5]

That is what Hyundai says, and it is all this page carries for 2026. No 2026 IONIQ 5 features grid was found on Hyundai's own newsroom to confirm the Heat Pump row directly.

## Hyundai's manuals confirm the car is built both ways

Hyundai's Ioniq 5 Owner's Manual gives the air conditioning system two refrigerant charges, one for each build: "Heat pump" 900±25 g (32±0.9 oz.) and "Without heat pump" 700±25 g (25±0.9 oz.), both R-1234yf. The compressor lubricant splits the same way, 180±10 g against 150±10 g. The table reads that way in the 2022 manual and again in the 2025 manual.[^6][^7]

## How this was checked

- Hyundai publishes two separate documents per model year, Specifications and Features, and only the Features document carries the HVAC grid. The word "pump" appears nowhere in Hyundai's 2022, 2023 or 2024 IONIQ 5 **Specifications** documents — but those documents contain no HVAC or interior section at all, so that silence is not evidence of anything and is not used here. Control: each of them does carry a "Battery Heating System" row, which is a different system.[^8]
- Every Heat Pump row quoted above was read from the PDF page rendered as an image, not from extracted text, because these are grid tables and text extraction reorders columns.
- Hyundai's press releases do not use the words. "Pump" occurs zero times in Hyundai's 2022 IONIQ 5 launch release and zero times in its September 2024 release announcing the 2025 range, with "battery" occurring 8 and 3 times respectively in the same searches.[^9][^10]

## Not covered here

The Ioniq 5 N is a different model and is not covered by any figure above. For what it is worth as a boundary marker, Hyundai's own 2026 IONIQ 5 N grid has a single column, headed "N AWD", and marks Heat Pump "S".[^11]

## Scope note

Model years 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025 are checked against Hyundai's own Features grid for each of those years, all four opened and read this pass. Model year 2026 rests on one document only, Hyundai's 2026 Model Year Changes, which calls the car a carry-over and lists no HVAC change; no 2026 IONIQ 5 features grid was located on hyundainews.com. That the search method can find a 2026-model-year grid when Hyundai has published one is shown by the two it did find, for the 2026 IONIQ 5 N and the 2026 IONIQ 6 N.

"AWD only" and "AWD-only" are Hyundai's exact cell values; the hyphen appears from the 2024 document onward. Hyundai's grids print no legend defining "S" or "-", so the reading of "S" as standard follows the grid's own use of it — every car in a column carries every "S" row — rather than a printed key.

The 2025 IONIQ 5 grid uses one SE column for both the SE and the SE Standard Range, and writes the difference out longhand where the two differ, as in its Trailering Package row. The Heat Pump cell is a plain "AWD-only", and the SE Standard Range is a rear-wheel-drive car, so it is excluded by the cell's own terms rather than by inference.

The 2025 IONIQ 5 Owner's Manual is a mirror hosted on cdn.dealereprocess.org, not on Hyundai's own host; it is the Hyundai-branded document, and it is used here only for a refrigerant-capacity table that the 2022 manual on Hyundai's own host states the same way.

The Ioniq 5 N is out of scope. Non-US Ioniq 5 builds are out of scope entirely; no non-US document was opened.
