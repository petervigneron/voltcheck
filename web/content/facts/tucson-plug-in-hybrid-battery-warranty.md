# Hyundai Tucson Plug-in Hybrid: Battery Warranty

## Hybrid, Plug-in Hybrid, and Electric Vehicle Warranty, all states

| Model year | Term | Repair or replacement restores at least |
|---|---|---|
| 2022[^6] | 10 years or 100,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^1] | 70% of the original battery capacity[^1] |
| 2023[^6] | 10 years or 100,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^2] | 70% of the original battery capacity[^2] |
| 2024[^6] | 10 years or 100,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^3] | 70% of the original battery capacity[^3] |
| 2025[^6] | 10 years or 100,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^4] | 70% of the original battery capacity[^4] |
| 2026[^6] | 10 years or 100,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^5] | 70% of the original battery capacity[^5] |

- Coverage name: "Hybrid, Plug-in Hybrid, and Electric Vehicle System Warranty" (2022 and 2023); "Hybrid, Plug-in Hybrid, and Electric Vehicle Warranty" (2024 through 2026).[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]
- The repair standard, each year: any repair or replacement "will maintain at least equal energy capacity to the original Battery before the failure occurred but no less than 70% of the original battery capacity."[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]
- Each handbook states battery capacity two ways on printed p. 24. Under "this Battery Limited Warranty": loss of battery capacity or power over time or due to or resulting from battery usage "is covered not to degrade more than 70% of the original battery capacity." Under the handbook's What Is Not Covered: "Loss of battery energy or power over time or due to or resulting from battery usage, is NOT covered under this Hybrid, Plug-in Hybrid, and Electric Vehicle Warranty" ("… System Warranty" in 2022 and 2023).[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]
- 2026, Hyundai's Tucson Plug-in Hybrid web page, footnote to "Hybrid/Electric Battery": "Coverage does not apply to lease and commercial vehicles or vehicles serviced or registered outside the U.S."[^7]

## Plug-in Hybrid Battery on California-certified cars, 2022 through 2025

| Model year | Plug-in Hybrid Battery | Cars it applies to | States named |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 10 years or 150,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^1] | California Emissions Vehicles certified as a transitional zero-emission vehicle (TZEV)[^1] | California, Connecticut, Delaware, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Vermont, Washington; sold, registered and operated there[^1] |
| 2023 | 10 years or 150,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^2] | 2023 vehicles certified for sale in California and certified as TZEV or super ultra low emission vehicle (SULEV)[^2] | California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, Vermont, Washington; sold, registered and operated there[^2] |
| 2024 | 10 years or 150,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^3] | 2024 vehicles certified for sale in California and certified as TZEV[^3] | California, Colorado, Connecticut, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, Rhode Island, Vermont; registered there[^3] |
| 2025 | 10 years or 150,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^4] | 2025 vehicles certified for sale in California and certified as TZEV[^4] | The handbook prints two lists; see below[^4] |

- Each year's California parts-list NOTE 4 (printed p. 41) gives the Plug-in Hybrid Battery's 10 years or 150,000 miles "under the California Emission Warranty".[^1][^2][^3][^4]
- Each year's term above is the Plug-in Hybrid Battery's, and is "not 15 years or 150,000 miles", the term the same California section gives the other listed emission parts of a TZEV-certified Tucson Plug-in Hybrid.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
- 2025, printed p. 40, Warranty Jurisdiction of the California emission warranty: California, Colorado, Connecticut, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Minnesota, Nevada, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, Rhode Island, Vermont, Virginia, Washington.[^4]
- 2025, printed p. 41, the parts list's NOTE 4, for the TZEV-certified 2025 Tucson Plug-in Hybrid: California, Colorado, Connecticut, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, Rhode Island, Vermont.[^4]

## Coverage at a glance

Model years 2022 through 2026:

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| Covered components | High Voltage Battery, along with the components directly attached to or integral to its operation: Battery Management System; Blower Assembly; Electronic Air Compressor; Power Relay Assembly; Hybrid Starter & Generator; Traction Motor including housing case; Hybrid Power Control Unit; OBC; Electric Power Control unit[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5] |
| Clock starts | Date of original retail delivery or date of first use[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5] |
| Battery repair | If possible, the battery components are repaired or replaced and the original battery is returned to the vehicle; if necessary, the battery is replaced with a new or refurbished battery[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5] |
| Parts used | New Hyundai Genuine Parts or Hyundai authorized remanufactured parts[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5] |
| Towing | To the nearest Hyundai Dealership or authorized service location, when the vehicle is inoperable due to a warrantable defect within 10 years or 100,000 miles, whichever occurs first[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5] |

## Transfer, in the handbook's words

- Warranty Transferability, printed p. 18, identical each year: "The New Vehicle Limited, Anti-Perforation Limited, Federal Emission Performance, Federal Emission Design and Defect, California Emission Control Systems, and Replacement Parts and Accessories Limited warranty coverage described in this handbook apply to the vehicle regardless of a change in ownership, and are transferable to subsequent owners. The 10 years/100,000 miles Powertrain Limited Warranty is not transferable and applies only to the original owner …"[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]
- The Summary of Warranty Coverage, printed p. 16, marks the Powertrain row "(ORIGINAL OWNER)", with the footnote "Original Owner 10 Years/100,000 Miles, Subsequent Owner(s) 5 Years/60,000 Miles". The row for this coverage ("Hybrid, Plug-in Hybrid, and Electric Vehicle System" in 2022 and 2023; "… Direct Energy Components" in 2024 through 2026) reads "10 years/100,000 miles".[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]

## What is not covered

The handbooks' Hybrid, Plug-in Hybrid, and Electric Vehicle section, printed pp. 23–24, excludes:[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]

- Damage resulting from intentional actions (including intentionally abusing or destroying your vehicle or ignoring active vehicle warnings), a collision or accident, or the servicing or opening of the Battery by non-authorized personnel.
- 2022 and 2023: "Physically damaging the Battery, or intentionally attempting either by physical means, programming, or other methods, to extend (other than as specified in your owner documentation) or reduce the life of the Battery." 2024 through 2026: "Physical Battery damage, or intentional damage by physical means, programming, or other methods, to extend (other than as specified in your owner documentation) or reduce the life of the Battery."
- Exposing the Battery to direct flame.
- Flooding of the Battery.
- Loss of battery energy or power over time or due to or resulting from battery usage.
- Physically damaging the battery, or intentionally attempting, either by physical means, programming, or other methods, to extend (other than as specified in the Owner's Manual provided with the Vehicle at the time of first retail purchase) or reduce the life of the battery.
- Exposing the battery to direct flame ("such as a result of any body repairs").
- Items not covered by, and exclusions and limitations of, the "What is Not Covered" section of the Hyundai New Vehicle Limited Warranty.
- Incidental or consequential damages associated with a vehicle failure, including without limitation loss of time; inconvenience; cost of transportation; telephone calls and lodging; loss of personal or commercial property; loss of pay or revenue; and loss of use of the vehicle.
