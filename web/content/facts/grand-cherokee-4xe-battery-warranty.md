# Jeep Grand Cherokee 4xe: Battery Warranty

## High voltage battery coverage

| Model year | California and the states listed below | All other states |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 10 years or 150,000 miles[^1] | 8 years or 100,000 miles[^1] |
| 2023 | 10 years or 150,000 miles[^2] | 8 years or 100,000 miles[^2] |
| 2024 | 10 years or 150,000 miles[^3] | 8 years or 100,000 miles[^3] |
| 2025 | 10 years or 150,000 miles[^4] | 8 years or 100,000 miles[^4] |
| 2026 | 8 years or 100,000 miles[^5] | 8 years or 100,000 miles[^5] |

- For 2022 through 2025, the longer term applies to a car certified for sale and registered in California or a listed state.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
- Jeep's November 2023 printing of the 2024 booklet gives the 10-year, 150,000-mile term to a car certified as a Transitional Zero Emission Vehicle (TZEV) and sold, registered and operated in California; its summary chart labels the same term "TZEV States".[^6]

| 2023 | California, Colorado, Connecticut, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, Rhode Island, Vermont[^2] |

## Coverage at a glance

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who is covered | A purchaser for use of the vehicle[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5] |
| Clock starts | The start date of the Basic Limited Warranty[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5] |
| That start date | The earlier of the date you take delivery and the date the vehicle was first put into use (2026: "into service"), for example as a dealer "demo" or an FCA US LLC company vehicle[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5] |
| Gradual capacity loss, 2024 through 2026 | Not covered[^3][^4][^5] |
| Parts used in FCA US LLC's exchange service | New, remanufactured, reconditioned or repaired, depending on the part, with the same warranties as new parts[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5] |

## Battery pack warranty extension

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| Which cars | Select 2022 through 2026 Grand Cherokee with the 2.0L I4 turbo PHEV engine; a dealer's VIN lookup shows whether a car is included[^8] |
| Term | Unlimited years and unlimited miles from the vehicle's in-service date[^8] |
| What it covers | The high voltage battery pack, for a fault that sets one of five battery diagnostic codes FCA US LLC names: cell balancing performance, pack voltage variation, battery state of health failed, and battery "A" or "B" voltage unstable[^8] |
| Any other warranty-qualifying battery issue | Covered under the standard high voltage battery warranty[^8] |
| Limit once the standard battery warranty has expired | FCA US LLC may cap the payment at the lower of the battery pack's replacement cost and the vehicle's retail value before the fault codes appeared, taken from the current NADA Used Car Pricing Guide for the car's location, mileage and condition, or from another nationally published guide if NADA's is not available[^8] |

## What is not covered

Jeep's 2024 and 2025 booklets exclude the costs of repairing damage or conditions caused by:

- Physically damaging the high voltage lithium-ion battery or intentionally attempting to reduce its life.[^3][^4]
- Leaving the vehicle for over 30 days where the high voltage lithium-ion battery reaches a zero or near zero state of charge.[^3][^4]
- Neglecting to follow correct charging procedures as recommended in the Owner's Manual.[^3][^4]
- Use of incompatible charging devices as recommended in the Owner's Manual.[^3][^4]
- Opening the high voltage lithium-ion battery enclosure or having it serviced by someone other than an authorized repair facility certified technician.[^3][^4]
- Exposing the high voltage lithium-ion battery to contact with a direct flame.[^3][^4]
- Exposing the vehicle to ambient temperatures above 140°F (60°C) for over 24 hours.[^3][^4]

Jeep's 2026 booklet excludes the costs of repairing damage or conditions caused by:

- Physically damaging the high voltage lithium-ion battery or intentionally attempting to reduce its life.[^5]
- Leaving the vehicle for over 30 days where the high voltage lithium-ion battery reaches a zero or near zero state of charge.[^5]
- Using the high voltage lithium-ion battery as a power source.[^5]
- Neglecting to follow correct charging procedures as recommended in the Owner's Manual.[^5]
- Use of incompatible charging devices as recommended in the Owner's Manual.[^5]
- Opening the high voltage lithium-ion battery enclosure or having it serviced by someone other than an authorized Chrysler, Dodge, Jeep or Ram repair facility.[^5]
- Exposing the high voltage lithium-ion battery to contact with a direct flame.[^5]
- Exposing the high voltage lithium-ion battery to ambient temperatures above 140°F (60°C) for over 24 hours.[^5]

| Model year | Federal Emission Warranty, specified components | FCA US LLC Limited Emission Warranty | California Emission Warranty |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 8 years or 80,000 miles[^1] | 8 years or 100,000 miles[^1] | 15 years or 150,000 miles for a TZEV sold, registered and operated in California[^7] |
| 2023 | 8 years or 80,000 miles[^2] | 8 years or 100,000 miles on the summary chart; 10 years or 150,000 miles on the "4xe Vehicles" parts in section 5.1 B[^2] | 15 years or 150,000 miles for a TZEV sold, registered and operated in California[^9] |
| 2024 | 8 years or 80,000 miles[^3] | 8 years or 100,000 miles, PHEV only[^3] | 15 years or 150,000 miles for a TZEV sold, registered and operated in California[^3] |

- For 2023, 2024 and 2025, Jeep states that for the High Voltage Battery Limited Warranty on a TZEV sold, registered and operated in California, the California Emission Performance and Defects warranty is 10 years or 150,000 miles.[^9][^3][^4]
