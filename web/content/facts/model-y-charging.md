# Tesla Model Y: Charging

## DC fast charging

- Tesla states its Supercharge figure as a distance rather than a time-to-80%, and it publishes **two** such figures for the Model Y at different scopes. They do not agree, and this page prints both.
- Model-level, from Tesla's support site, under Tesla's own framing "Charging speeds vary slightly by model": "Model Y Up to 162 miles in 15 minutes." Tesla attaches no trim to that row; it is the Model Y as a nameplate. The rest of that five-row table: Model S "Up to 200 miles in 15 minutes," Model 3 and Model X "Up to 175 miles in 15 minutes," Cybertruck "Up to 137 miles in 15 minutes."[^1]
- Per-trim, from Tesla's own compare page, for the one Model Y trim it configures: Model Y Rear-Wheel Drive, "Up to 160 mi," "Supercharge in 15 min."[^8]
- The compare page states the test basis the support page leaves out. Tesla's own footnote to that 160: "Range added in 15 minutes is based on vehicle efficiency at a constant highway speed of 65 mph. Actual driving range and charging performance may vary depending on several factors including Supercharger type, vehicle configuration, state of charge, battery temperature, speed, weather and elevation change, among others."[^8]
- Tesla nowhere reconciles the two. 162 is the nameplate figure and 160 is the figure Tesla prints against a named trim, so the lower one is the one tied to a specific car — which is why this page does not print 162 alone.[^1][^8]
- Peak rate at a V3 Supercharger. Tesla's own words: "V3 Superchargers are capable of delivering peak charge rates up to 250 kW."[^1]
- A V4 Supercharger does not make a Model Y faster. Tesla's own words, in two consecutive paragraphs of one answer: "In North America, V4 Superchargers can charge up to 250 kW for Model S, Model 3, Model X and Model Y vehicles. … Cybertruck vehicles have the ability to charge up to 325 kW only at V4 Superchargers."[^1]
- Tesla says plainly that 250 kW is not a rate every car will see. Tesla's own words, to its own question "Will all Tesla vehicles be able to receive a 250 kW charge?": "No. Charging rates vary based on several factors such as size and age of battery pack, state of charge, ambient temperature conditions and vehicle configuration."[^1]
- Tesla publishes no 10%-to-80% charge time for the Model Y in any document cited here, and no per-trim DC peak in kilowatts. Neither is stated on this page.

## AC (Level 2, home) charging

- Tesla's Wall Connector tops out at 11.5 kW. Tesla's own words: "Wall Connector can provide up to 11.5 kW / 48 amp output. For Tesla vehicles, Wall Connector provides up to 44 miles of range per hour of charge."[^2]
- The breaker you install is not the amperage the connector delivers, and Tesla's table gives both. Its first two columns are headed "Circuit breaker (amps)" and "Maximum output (amps)," and they pair up: a 60 A breaker gives 48 A output, 50 A gives 40 A, 40 A gives 32 A, 30 A gives 24 A, 20 A gives 16 A, 15 A gives 12 A.[^2]
- Tesla's own instruction for picking one. Tesla's words: "For the fastest home charging, install Wall Connector with a circuit breaker that matches your vehicle's onboard charger capabilities. To view the max amperage of your Tesla vehicle, tap the lightning bolt icon on the touchscreen." And, for a panel that cannot spare 60 A: "For unique power situations or when power may be limited, Wall Connector can also be installed with lower amperage circuit breakers to support almost any existing electrical system."[^2]
- Model Y range gained per hour of charge, from Tesla's own Wall Connector table, **by circuit breaker**: 60 A (48 A output, 11.5 kW) gives 44 mph; 50 A (40 A, 9.6 kW) gives 37 mph; 40 A (32 A, 7.7 kW) gives 30 mph; 30 A (24 A, 5.7 kW) gives 22 mph; 20 A (16 A, 3.8 kW) gives 15 mph; 15 A (12 A, 2.8 kW) gives 11 mph. Tesla's own footnote on that table: "All charge speeds are approximate."[^2]
- Tesla caps two Model Y drivetrains below the top of its own table. Tesla's own footnote to it, verbatim and in full: "Maximum charge rate for Model 3 Rear-Wheel Drive, Model Y Rear-Wheel Drive and Model Y All-Wheel Drive is 32 A (7.7 kW) - up to 30 miles of range per hour."[^2]
- What that removes is the top of the list, not the list. 32 A output is the 40 A breaker row, so on a Model Y Rear-Wheel Drive or All-Wheel Drive the 40 A row and every row below it still apply exactly as printed; only the 60 A and 50 A rows — the 44 mph and 37 mph figures — do not.[^2]
- So on those two drivetrains a 60 A circuit gains the same 30 miles per hour as a 40 A one, and the two larger breakers buy nothing.[^2]
- Tesla does not say which Model Ys the footnote's two drivetrain names cover, and gives no model-year range or trim list for it. Read it as Tesla's current wording about drivetrains, not as a settled statement about a particular car — the touchscreen's lightning-bolt icon, which Tesla points to above, is what answers it for a specific vehicle.[^2]
- Tesla's figures for wall outlets, stated for Tesla vehicles generally rather than for the Model Y specifically: "A 120 volt outlet will supply 2 to 3 miles of range per hour of charge," and "a 240 volt outlet will supply up to 30 miles of range per hour of charge."[^3]

## Charge port and adapters

- The charge port is on the driver's side, at the rear. Tesla's own words: "The charge port is located on the left side of Model Y, behind a door that is part of the rear tail light assembly."[^4]
- Charging anywhere other than a Tesla connector needs an adapter. Tesla's own words: "To charge at a public charging station, plug the appropriate adapter into the vehicle's charging port, and then connect the station's charging connector to the adapter."[^4]
- For a CCS1 DC station, the adapter is Tesla's CCS Combo 1 Adapter, rated 500V DC. Tesla's own words: "Use only to connect the charge cable on a CCS Combo 1 charging station to a Tesla vehicle that is capable of Combo 1 DC charging."[^5]
- Not every Model Y can use it. Tesla's own words: "Not all Tesla vehicles are equipped with CCS charging capability. To determine if your vehicle is compatible, touch Controls > Software > Additional Vehicle Information on the touchscreen, or contact Tesla."[^5]
- The same touchscreen page answers a second question a used-car shopper cares about. Tesla directs the same "Controls > Software > Additional Vehicle Information" path to find out whether a given car has a heat pump.[^4]
- For an AC Level 2 station on the J1772 standard, a separate adapter is needed. Tesla's own words: "Charging stations labeled as J1772 will work with a SAE J1772 adapter and 120 volt stations labeled as 120 volt will work with the Mobile Connector."[^3]

## Preconditioning

- Preconditioning for a Supercharger is automatic, triggered by navigation, with nothing for the driver to switch on. Tesla's own words: "Your vehicle preconditions its high voltage Battery when navigating to Supercharger. This ensures you arrive with an optimal Battery temperature, reducing the amount of time it takes to charge."[^6]
- It works for non-Tesla fast chargers too, by the same route. Tesla's own words: "To precondition the Battery for a third-party fast charger, search for the fast charger in your vehicle's navigation and select it to enter it as the destination. Your vehicle automatically recognizes the selected destination as a fast charger and preconditions its Battery accordingly."[^6]
- Third-party stations are hidden by default. Tesla's own words: "To show charging stations that are not owned and operated by Tesla on the charger list and map, touch Controls > Navigation > Third-Party Charging Stations. This option is disabled by default."[^6]
- Arriving cold costs time at the plug. Tesla's own words, on what happens when a connector is inserted: the car "Heats or cools the Battery, if needed. If the Battery requires heating or cooling, you may notice a delay before charging begins."[^4]
- Home preconditioning is separate and is scheduled, not automatic. Tesla's own words: "If you don't schedule a Precondition, Model Y only warms the Battery before charging if the Battery is too cold to charge, and doesn't prepare the cabin climate."[^7]
- Scheduled charging does not apply at a Supercharger. Tesla's own words: "Scheduled charge cannot be used with fast chargers, including Tesla Superchargers. Charging schedules you create are ignored when you charge at a fast charger."[^7]

## Scope note

Tesla **does** version its owner's manual by model year, in broad editions rather than one file per year: the Model Y manual is served as a "2020-2024" edition at `/ownersmanual/2020_2024_modely/` and a "2025+" edition at `/ownersmanual/modely/`. Most Model Ys for sale used are pre-2024 cars, so the edition that covers them is cited alongside the current one in every manual footnote above. Each quoted line was read in **both** editions on 2026-08-25 and reads identically in both; where a quote appears in only one, it is not on this page. The sibling Model Y heat-pump sheet cites the same two editions.

Tesla's support pages are a different matter: they carry no edition, no version and no date, and Tesla revises them in place. Every support-page footnote above therefore carries the date it was read, 2026-08-25, and nothing read from those pages should be assumed still current later.

Every Model Y quotation above was read from the Model Y edition of Tesla's manual, not from the Model 3 edition. Tesla serves the two under separate paths and the text differs where the cars differ.

Tesla's host blocks non-browser clients, so every tesla.com page above was opened in a real browser session. The CCS Combo 1 Adapter manual is a PDF served from Tesla's own digitalassets.tesla.com host and was fetched directly.

Two things a shopper may expect and will not find here, because Tesla does not publish them in any document cited: a 10%-to-80% charge time for the Model Y, and a per-trim DC fast-charge peak in kilowatts. Tesla publishes a miles-per-15-minutes figure and a Supercharger-side peak instead, and those are what this page carries.

Where Tesla publishes two Supercharge figures at different scopes — 162 miles for the nameplate on its support site, 160 for the Rear-Wheel Drive trim on its compare page — both are printed above with their scoping, and neither is presented as the other's correction. Tesla does not say which supersedes which.

The AC charge-rate footnote naming Model Y Rear-Wheel Drive and Model Y All-Wheel Drive is Tesla's current wording. Tesla does not say which model years or which named trims those drivetrain descriptions cover, so no year range and no trim list is attached to it here. It is quoted in full above rather than paraphrased, because what it caps is the top two rows of Tesla's table and not the table.
