# Ford Mustang Mach-E: Charging

## DC fast charging

| Model year | DC fast charging | Battery, Ford's usable capacity |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 | Up to 150 kW[^1] | 68 kWh Standard Range, 88 kWh Extended Range[^1] |
| 2022 | Up to 150 kW[^2] | 70 kWh Standard Range, 91 kWh Extended Range[^2] |
| 2023 | Up to 150 kW[^3] | 70 kWh Standard Range, 91 kWh Extended Range[^3] |
| 2024 | Up to 150 kW[^4] | 72 kWh Standard Range, 91 kWh Extended Range[^4] |
| 2025 | Up to 150 kW[^5] | 73 kWh Standard Range or 88 kWh Extended Range on Select and Premium, 91 kWh Extended Range on GT[^6] |


## Connector and adapters

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| Charge port, 2021 to 2025 | SAE J1772 CCS, charging on 120V, 240V and DC fast charge[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5] |
| Factory NACS port | Announced January 31, 2024 for a transition from 2025[^7], but Ford's 2025 order guide still lists the SAE J1772 CCS port[^5] |
| Ford Fast Charging Adapter (NACS) | Part VRK9Z10E826A, $200.00[^8] |
| Adapter rating | Up to 500 amps and 1,000 volts, IP67, -22°F to 122°F[^8] |
| Adapter warranty | 3 years from the purchase date[^8] |
| Third-party adapters | Only NACS DC adapters sold or provided by Ford or Tesla; Tesla prohibits any other adapter at its stations[^9] |
| Where the adapter works | Designated Tesla Superchargers in the United States and Canada[^10] |
| What the adapter does not do | Level 1 or Level 2 AC charging, including Tesla Destination Chargers and home chargers with NACS connectors[^8] |

- Not all Tesla Superchargers are compatible with non-Tesla EVs, and Ford directs owners to check compatibility in the Ford app.[^10]
- Some Supercharger site layouts require a Ford to occupy two parking spaces for the cable to reach.[^10]
- Ford warns that not following its adapter instructions may cause vehicle damage not covered by the vehicle warranty.[^9]

## Home charging (Level 2)

| Model year | Onboard AC charger |
|---|---|
| 2021 | Up to 10.5 kW, 48 amps[^1] |
| 2022 | Up to 10.5 kW, 48 amps[^2] |
| 2023 | Up to 10.5 kW, 48 amps[^3] |
| 2024 | Up to 11 kW, 48 amps[^4] |
| 2025 | Up to 11 kW, 48 amps[^5] |

Ford's figures for a 2025 Mustang Mach-E, 0% to 100%:

| Equipment | 0% to 100% | Average miles per charging hour |
|---|---|---|
| Ford Mobile Power Cord | Est. 11.7 hours[^11] | Est. 22 miles[^11] |
| Ford Charge Station Pro | Est. 7.8 hours[^11] | Est. 33.3 miles[^11] |

- The included power cord has a switchable cord end: 120V (NEMA 5-15) up to 15 amps, or 240V (NEMA 14-50) up to 32 amps.[^3]

## Scope note

Model years 2021 through 2025, each checked against Ford's own order guide for that year; model year 2026 was not checked and is not claimed. Model year 2025 has two revisions and both are used: the charging lines are identical in each, and only the later 04/28/25 revision publishes battery capacities rather than "TBD". The adapter, Supercharger and preconditioning facts come from Ford pages that describe Ford's EV lineup rather than the Mustang Mach-E alone.
