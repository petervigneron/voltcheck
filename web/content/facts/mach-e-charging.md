# Ford Mustang Mach-E (model years 2021–2025): Charging

## DC fast charging

- Ford's order guide states the same DC figure in every model year from 2021 through 2025: "DC Charging: Up to 150kW capability."[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]
- Ford does not publish a separate DC rate for the Standard Range pack in any of those five order guides. One figure covers the car. No Standard Range figure is claimed here, because no Ford document opened for this sheet states one.
- That line sits in the Select trim's standard-equipment list, and the higher trims inherit it: Ford's Premium page opens "ALL SELECT SERIES EQUIPMENT PLUS:" and adds to it rather than restating it.[^4]
- Battery, in Ford's own usable-capacity wording, by model year:
  - 2021: 68 kWh Standard Range, 88 kWh Extended Range.[^1]
  - 2022: 70 kWh Standard Range, 91 kWh Extended Range.[^2]
  - 2023: 70 kWh Standard Range, 91 kWh Extended Range.[^3]
  - 2024: 72 kWh Standard Range, 91 kWh Extended Range.[^4]
  - 2025: 91 kWh Extended Range. The Standard Range capacity is printed as "TBD" throughout the October 22, 2024 order guide.[^5]
- Time to charge 10% to 80%: not covered. None of the Ford documents opened for this sheet states a 10-80% time for any Mach-E trim or model year.

## Connector

- Every Mustang Mach-E, 2021 through 2025, has an "SAE J1772 CCS (Combo Connector System) Charge Port capable of charging on 120V, 240V and Direct Current (DC Fast Charge)."[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]
- Ford announced on January 31, 2024 that a Fast Charging Adapter "will provide Ford F-150 Lightning, and Mustang Mach-E owners access to Tesla's V3 and above Superchargers," and that "Ford will start to transition to the NACS charge port from 2025, removing the need for an adapter."[^6] Ford's own 2025 Mustang Mach-E order guide still lists the SAE J1772 CCS port, not a NACS port.[^5]
- The Ford Fast Charging Adapter (NACS), part number VRK9Z10E826A, sells for $200.00 on Ford's accessories site. Ford rates it for up to 500 amps and 1,000 volts, IP67 weatherproofing, and operation from -22°F to 122°F, with a 3-year warranty from the purchase date.[^7]
- The adapter is for DC fast charging only. Ford states it is "Not compatible with Level 1 or Level 2 AC chargers, including Tesla Destination Chargers and home chargers with NACS connectors."[^7]
- Ford does not endorse third-party adapters: "only use NACS DC adapters sold or provided by Ford or Tesla for use at Tesla Superchargers. Tesla prohibits the use of any other adapter with Tesla charging stations." Ford adds that failing to follow those instructions "may result in vehicle damage not covered by the vehicle warranty."[^8]
- Not every Tesla Supercharger works. Ford's words: "Not all Tesla Superchargers are compatible with non-Tesla EVs," and Ford directs owners to check compatibility in the Ford app. Some Supercharger site layouts require a Ford to occupy two parking spaces for the cable to reach.[^9]

## AC (Level 2, home) charging

- Onboard AC charger, from Ford's order guides: "Up to 10.5kW with 48 Amps capability" for model years 2021, 2022 and 2023;[^1][^2][^3] "Up to 11kW with 48 Amps capability" for 2024 and 2025.[^4][^5]
- Ford's estimates for charging a 2025 Mustang Mach-E from 0% to 100%: 11.7 hours with the Ford Mobile Power Cord, an average of 22 miles per charging hour; 7.8 hours with the Ford Charge Station Pro, an average of 33.3 miles per charging hour.[^10]
- The included power cord has a "Switchable cord end to allow customer charge functionality with 120V (NEMA 5-15) up to 15 Amps or 240V (NEMA 14-50) up to 32 Amps."[^3]

## Preconditioning

- Ford states that battery preconditioning runs off navigation: "with available Connected Navigation you can precondition your vehicle's battery on the way to the charger."[^10] Ford's wording is "available," not standard.

## Scope note

Model years 2021 through 2025, checked directly against Ford's own order guide for each year. The 2021 guide was read through a reader proxy pointed at a forum mirror of Ford's file, because the mirror refuses a direct fetch; every other guide was downloaded as Ford's own PDF from the mirror named in its footnote. Model year 2026 was not checked and is not claimed.

The adapter, Supercharger and preconditioning facts come from Ford pages that are not model-year specific. They are current as of the date this sheet was last checked, and describe Ford's EV lineup, not the Mustang Mach-E alone.
