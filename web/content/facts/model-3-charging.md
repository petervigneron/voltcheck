# Tesla Model 3: Charging

## DC fast charging

| Figure | Answer |
|---|---|
| Supercharge, Model 3 as a nameplate | Up to 175 miles in 15 minutes[^1] |
| Supercharge, Model 3 Rear-Wheel Drive | Up to 170 miles in 15 minutes[^2] |
| Peak rate, V3 Supercharger | Up to 250 kW[^1] |
| Peak rate, V4 Supercharger | Up to 250 kW, the same as V3, for Model S, Model 3, Model X and Model Y; only Cybertruck charges up to 325 kW[^1] |
| 10% to 80% charge time | Not published[^1][^2] |
| Per-trim DC peak in kilowatts | Not published[^1][^2] |

- Tesla's basis for the 15-minute figure: vehicle efficiency at a constant highway speed of 65 mph, with actual range and charging performance varying by Supercharger type, vehicle configuration, state of charge, battery temperature, speed, weather and elevation change.[^2]
- Not every car reaches 250 kW: Tesla says rates vary with the size and age of the battery pack, state of charge, ambient temperature and vehicle configuration.[^1]

## Home charging (Level 2)

| Circuit breaker | Maximum output | Miles of range per hour on a Tesla Wall Connector |
|---|---|---|
| 60 A | 48 A, 11.5 kW | 44[^3] |
| 50 A | 40 A, 9.6 kW | 37[^3] |
| 40 A | 32 A, 7.7 kW | 30[^3] |
| 30 A | 24 A, 5.7 kW | 22[^3] |
| 20 A | 16 A, 3.8 kW | 15[^3] |
| 15 A | 12 A, 2.8 kW | 11[^3] |

- All charge speeds in Tesla's table are approximate.[^3]
- Trim cap: the maximum charge rate for Model 3 Rear-Wheel Drive is 32 A (7.7 kW), up to 30 miles of range per hour — the 40 A breaker row, so a 60 A or 50 A circuit buys that trim nothing.[^3]
- To match a breaker to a specific car, Tesla points to the lightning bolt icon on the touchscreen, which shows the car's maximum amperage.[^3]
- A Wall Connector can also be installed on lower-amperage breakers where household power is limited.[^3]
- Wall outlets, stated for Tesla vehicles generally: a 120 volt outlet supplies 2 to 3 miles of range per hour, and a 240 volt outlet up to 30.[^4]

## Connector and adapters

| Item | Answer |
|---|---|
| Charge port location | Left side of the car, behind a door that is part of the rear tail light assembly[^5] |
| Native connector | Tesla; any other public station needs an adapter plugged into the car's port first[^5] |
| DC CCS1 stations | Tesla CCS Combo 1 Adapter, rated 500V DC[^6] |
| CCS capability | Not on every Model 3 — check Controls, Software, Additional Vehicle Information on the touchscreen, or contact Tesla[^6] |
| AC J1772 stations | SAE J1772 adapter[^4] |
| 120 volt stations | Mobile Connector[^4] |

- That same touchscreen page, Controls, Software, Additional Vehicle Information, is also where Tesla says to look for whether a car has a heat pump.[^5]

## Preconditioning

| Situation | What the car does |
|---|---|
| Navigating to a Supercharger | Preconditions the high voltage battery automatically, so it arrives at an optimal temperature[^7] |
| Navigating to a third-party fast charger | The same, once the charger is entered as the navigation destination[^7] |
| Third-party stations on the map | Disabled by default; enable under Controls, Navigation, Third-Party Charging Stations[^7] |
| Plugging in cold | Heats or cools the battery if needed, which may delay the start of charging[^5] |
| At home with no schedule set | Warms the battery only if it is too cold to charge, and does not prepare the cabin climate[^8] |
| At a fast charger with a schedule set | Charging schedules are ignored, including at Tesla Superchargers[^8] |
