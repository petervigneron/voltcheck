## Connector and adapters

| Model year | Port |
|---|---|
| 2022–2024 | Native Combined Charging System (CCS), not NACS[^8] |
| 2025, excluding the Ioniq 5 N | Native NACS — the first Hyundai model to ship with the Tesla North American Charging Standard port[^9][^10] |
| 2025 Ioniq 5 N | CCS, not NACS[^11][^12] |

### Adapter direction 1: 2022 to 2024 CCS1-port Ioniq 5 at a Tesla Supercharger

- Requires a Hyundai-issued CCS-to-NACS adapter, which Hyundai says is essential for reliable charging at V3 Tesla Superchargers and other NACS DC fast charging stations.[^13]
- Free to owners and lessees who purchased or leased on or before January 31, 2025, claimed through a MyHyundai account.[^13]
- Bought after that date: MSRP $250, with the dealer setting the actual selling price at 100% discretion.[^14]
- Non-Hyundai adapters are not described as prohibited, but Hyundai's warranty will not cover damage caused by non-Hyundai accessories; Hyundai says to use only adapters with CSA or UL certification.[^15]

### Adapter direction 2: 2025 native-NACS Ioniq 5 at a CCS1 station

- A CCS adapter is included with the 25MY Ioniq 5 at delivery; the customer does not order it.[^16]
- Its purpose is access to charging networks such as Electrify America and EVgo that currently only support the CCS standard.[^17]
- The 2025 owner's manual documents "Using the CCS1 DC Charging Adapter," for use when the public station's charger is a CCS1 charger.[^18]
- If the adapter is missing at delivery, Hyundai directs the owner to their dealer for a free replacement.[^19]
